Foie Gras Poêlé aux RaisinsPan-Seared Foie Gras with Grapes

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep30 min Cook35 min servings2

A warm appetizer of duck foie gras seared to a savory brown crust and paired with grapes and a sweet-wine sauce. Prepare the sauce and toast first, then finish by warming the grapes in the rendered foie gras fat. This version is designed for the Japanese domestic audience and cooks the center through; it does not aim for a rare center.

Method

115 min

Prepare grapes, aromatics and accompaniments

  • 1 Detach 180 g white Muscat grapes from their stems. Immerse 90 g in boiling water for 10 seconds, transfer to ice water and peel with your fingers.
  • 2 Leave the remaining 90 g unpeeled and dry with paper towels.
  • 3 Peel 1 shallot, about 25 g, and mince very finely, or ciseler.
  • 4 Slice 3 g chives into 2 mm pieces.
  • 5 Wash 20 g lamb’s lettuce in cold water, spin completely dry in a salad spinner and refrigerate.
  • 6 Measure 80 ml Sauternes, 120 ml brown chicken stock, 20 ml verjuice and 15 ml Armagnac separately and arrange beside the stove.
  • 7 Cut 20 g unsalted butter into 1 cm cubes and return to the refrigerator to keep cold.

Ready when

The grapes are divided into 90 g peeled and 90 g unpeeled, with all liquids and butter assembled beside the stove.

210 min

Trim and chill the foie gras

  • 1 Use the tip of a knife to remove large blood vessels visible on the cut surfaces of the 2 chilled foie gras slices, keeping the flesh intact.
  • 2 Pat dry, arrange on a clean tray, cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate until just before searing.

Ready when

There are 2 chilled escalopes, 1.5–2 cm thick and about 80 g each.

Caution

Leaving foie gras at room temperature too long softens its fat.

36 min

Make the sweet-wine sauce base

  • 1 Melt 10 g butter in a small saucepan and sauté the minced shallot for 1 minute 30 seconds.
  • 2 Turn off the heat briefly and pour in 15 ml Armagnac.
  • 3 Return to medium heat and boil for 40–50 seconds to remove the alcohol smell.
  • 4 Pour in 80 ml Sauternes and deglaze, scraping the bottom with a wooden spoon to dissolve the flavorful browned deposits in the liquid.
  • 5 Reduce over medium heat for 2–3 minutes until half the liquid remains.

Ready when

The browned deposits are completely dissolved and the liquid barely covers the pan’s bottom.

Caution

Do not ignite the spirit.

47 min

Reduce with stock and verjuice

  • 1 Pour in 120 ml brown chicken stock and reduce over medium heat for 4–5 minutes to about 60 ml.
  • 2 Add 20 ml verjuice, boil for 20 seconds, taste and season with 0.5 g fleur de sel.
  • 3 Turn off the heat, cover and keep warm. Do not add the remaining 10 g butter yet.

Ready when

Continue when the sauce is lightly thickened and no sharp alcohol smell remains.

54 min

Toast the bread bases

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile the bread toasts, warm 2 serving plates.
  • 2 Prepare 2 slices of country bread, 1 cm thick.
  • 3 Toast in a toaster oven or under a grill for 1 minute 30 seconds per side, until the edges are golden.

Ready when

The surfaces are dry, the edges golden, and the slices feel lightly firm when lifted.

65 min

Season and lightly flour the foie gras

  • 1 Preheat a cast-iron or heavy frying pan over medium heat without oil for 2 minutes 30 seconds.
  • 2 Meanwhile, take the 2 escalopes from the refrigerator and lightly blot both sides with paper towels to remove moisture and seeped fat.
  • 3 Season both sides evenly with 1.5 g fleur de sel and 0.5 g black pepper.
  • 4 Sift 8 g cake flour lightly over both sides and edges through a tea strainer, then brush off the excess by hand.

Ready when

Both sides have a faint white flour coating and a slight heat shimmer rises from the pan.

78 min

Brown and cook the foie gras through

  • 1 Set the preheated pan over medium-high heat and place the 2 escalopes apart in it without adding oil.
  • 2 Cook undisturbed for 45–60 seconds on the first side, then lift an edge with a spatula to check the color.
  • 3 Once browned, turn and lower the heat. Insert a clean thermometer from the side and ensure the center remains at 75°C or above for at least 1 minute.
  • 4 Transfer to a warmed tray and cover loosely with foil.
  • 5 Leave only about 2 tsp rendered fat in the pan, transferring the rest to a heatproof container.

Ready when

Continue when both sides are browned and the center has stayed at 75°C or above for at least 1 minute.

Caution

Do not judge whether the center is cooked from the surface browning alone.

84 min

Combine the grapes and sauce

  • 1 Return the foie gras pan to medium heat. Add 90 g unpeeled and 90 g peeled grapes and warm for 60 seconds, rolling them with a wooden spoon.
  • 2 Sprinkle over 5 g granulated sugar and shake the pan for another 60–90 seconds to glaze the grapes.
  • 3 Add the prepared warm sauce, stir and turn off the heat. Melt in the remaining 10 g butter and coat the grapes.

Ready when

The grapes are glossy, with very faint browned spots on their skins.

94 min

Assemble and finish the plates

  • 1 Place 1 slice of toast in the center of each warmed plate.
  • 2 Put 1 seared foie gras slice on each toast.
  • 3 Distribute grapes evenly around the foie gras and spoon sauce along the edges of the foie gras and bread.
  • 4 Place half of the 20 g lamb’s lettuce at the front of each plate.
  • 5 Scatter 1 g fleur de sel over the foie gras with your fingers and grind over 0.5 g black pepper.
  • 6 Sprinkle the 3 g sliced chives over everything.

Ready when

The seared foie gras surface is glossy and the sauce stays around its edges instead of spreading across the plate.

About this dish

A warm appetizer pairing foie gras with fruit brings seasonality to the French table. With southwestern France’s food culture in mind, grapes make a welcome autumn pairing.

For a dinner with guests, a small portion opens the meal. The menu offers foie gras in its warm expression, distinct from a cold preparation.

Chef Authen’s tip

Verjuice is tart juice obtained from unripe grapes. Although the fruit is the same, its starting point differs from wines and fruit dishes made after the grapes ripen.
