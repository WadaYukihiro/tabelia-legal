Method

110 min

Prepare the ingredients

  • 1 Finely chop the onion, carrot and celery, about 60g each.
  • 2 Finely chop the fresh pork belly or pancetta.
2

Sauté the soffritto

  • 1 Sauté the pork belly in 45ml extra virgin olive oil over medium heat.
  • 2 Add the onion, carrot and celery and sprinkle with a little salt.
  • 3 Sauté over medium heat for 10 minutes until the vegetables soften and develop sweetness.
38 min

Brown the meat

  • 1 Add the ground meat, either all beef or a blend of 60% beef and 40% pork, and cook over high heat, breaking it up.
  • 2 When it loses its pink color and turns brown, add 150ml wine and cook off the alcohol, about 2 minutes.
41 hr 40 min

Make the sauce

  • 1 Stir in 200g passata and 15g concentrated tomato paste, cover and simmer over low heat for 90–120 minutes.
  • 2 Add beef stock as needed if the liquid runs low during cooking.

Caution

Too much tomato takes the sauce away from its authentic flavor. Use it sparingly to support the meat rather than as the main ingredient.

540 min

Make the tagliatelle dough

  • 1 While the ragù simmers, mix 300g tipo 00 flour with 3 eggs to form a smooth dough, wrap in plastic wrap and rest for 30 minutes.
  • 2 MeanwhileStart bringing a large pot of water to a boil.

About this dish

Tagliatelle al ragù is Bologna’s signature dish and the original form of the meat sauce known around the world as Bolognese. However, a dish called “spaghetti Bolognese” does not exist in Bologna. At its source, ragù is always served with broad ribbons of fresh tagliatelle.

In 1982, the Bologna delegation of the Italian Academy of Cuisine registered an official recipe before a notary. Even the width of tagliatelle is specified: once cooked, it should equal 1/12270 of the height of Bologna’s symbolic Arginentaria Tower, at 97.7 meters—approximately 8 millimeters. In April 2023, the Academy updated the registered recipe following research by a specialist committee. Principal changes included increasing the ground beef from 300g to 400g, introducing passata and adopting fresh pork belly. The principle that ragù is meat sauce with tomato, rather than tomato sauce with meat, was set out explicitly.

A mixture of ground beef and pork, soffritto of onion, carrot and celery, wine and passata is simmered over low heat for at least 3 hours, ideally 4–5 hours.

Chef Authen’s tip

The rough surface of broad tagliatelle catches ragù, while spaghetti lets it slide off: this is a matter of physics. “Spaghetti Bolognese” is a dish name that does not exist in Bologna. Come to Bologna and share tagliatelle with ragù. Then see whether you can still say it is the same with spaghetti.
