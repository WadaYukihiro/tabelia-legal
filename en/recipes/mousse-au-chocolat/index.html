Mousse au ChocolatChocolate Mousse

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep10 min Cook14 min Rest3 hr servings4

The traditional French household mousse, built on eggs and chocolate. In French homes, the standard method uses no cream and relies solely on beaten egg whites for lightness. Nothing dilutes the flavor of 60–70% cocoa chocolate, so the precision of the meringue and gentle folding directly determine the texture. It can be prepared and chilled the day before, making an apt finish to a meal.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Chocolate (60–70% cocoa)Baking couverture is ideal. Chop finely and melt over a water bath.200 g
  • EggsAlways use fresh eggs because they are not cooked. Separate yolks and whites.4 eggs
  • Caster sugarFor the meringue; stabilizes the bubbles.30 g
  • SaltAdd to the whites to help them foam.1 pinch

Method

110 min

Melt the chocolate over a water bath

  • 1 Finely chop 200g chocolate into a bowl and set over a pan of water at 50–60°C to melt. When completely melted, glossy and smooth, remove from the water and cool to body temperature.
  • 2 Separate 4 eggs into yolks and whites. Yolks in the whites prevent the meringue from whipping, so separate 1 egg at a time into a small bowl before transferring.
23 min

Mix the yolks into the chocolate

  • 1 Add the 4 yolks to the chocolate cooled to body temperature, 1 at a time, mixing well after each.

Ready when

Proceed when the mixture is glossy, smooth and creamy.

Caution

Hot chocolate cooks the yolks and makes the mixture grainy, so always cool to body temperature before adding them.

36 min

Whip the egg whites into meringue

  • 1 Make the meringue.
  • 2 In a separate clean bowl, whip the 4 egg whites with 1 pinch salt. Once white foam covers the mixture, continue whipping while adding 30g caster sugar in 2 additions.
  • 3 Stop before overwhipping makes it grainy.

Ready when

It is ready when lifting the whisk produces peaks with tips that bow slightly.

45 min

Fold the meringue into the chocolate

  • 1 Add 1/3 of the meringue to the chocolate and whisk firmly to loosen it; the bubbles in this portion can be sacrificed.
  • 2 Add the remaining meringue in 2 additions, using a spatula to fold broadly up from the bottom of the bowl. Stop as soon as no white streaks remain. Overmixing destroys the bubbles and makes a heavy mousse.
53 hr

Chill to set

  • 1 Divide evenly among 4 dishes, cover with plastic wrap and refrigerate for 3 hours to set.

About this dish

Chocolate mousse is one of the desserts most often made in French homes. Mousse means “foam” in French, and early forms emerged in the 18th–19th centuries as chocolate expanded from a drink into a pastry ingredient, eventually joining the home-cooking repertoire.

The version without cream is said to have spread as the dish became established in bourgeois home cooking during the 20th century. A large bowl placed on the table for everyone to serve themselves has long been a familiar ending to birthdays and Sunday meals. The option of making it the day before is another reason for its popularity at home.

It is now also a bistro dessert staple, each establishment expressing its character through chocolate choice and firmness. Whether as a child’s first learned dessert or an adult’s choice after dinner, it remains at the table across generations.

Chef Authen’s tip

The standard chocolate mousse in French homes contains no cream, preserving chocolate flavor and relying on egg-white bubbles alone for lightness. Mousse means “foam” in French. It is an unusually honest dessert whose name itself describes its composition.
