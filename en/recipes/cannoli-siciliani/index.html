Authentic Sicilian CannoliCannoli Siciliani

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep1 hr 40 min Cook20 min Rest12 hr servings8

Sicily’s iconic fried pastry tubes, the Marsala-spiked shell rolled and fried, filled with sheep’s milk ricotta and chocolate chips just before serving. The filling must go in at the last moment.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (8 servings)

  • All-purpose flour250 g
  • Sugar30 g
  • Lard or unsalted butter30 g
  • Egg1
  • Dry Marsala wine80 ml
  • White wine vinegar10 ml
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder10 g
  • Salt1 g
  • Frying oil1000 ml
  • Fresh sheep's milk ricotta (drained)500 g
  • Powdered sugar200 g
  • Dark chocolate chips80 g
  • Candied citron30 g
  • Powdered sugar15 g

Method

112 hr

Drain the ricotta

Drain 500g ricotta in a colander overnight, about 12 hours. It’s ready once the liquid has fully drained off — excess moisture will make the cream collapse, so start this the day before.

21 hr 10 min

Make and rest the dough

Mix and knead the flour, sugar, lard, egg, Marsala, vinegar, cocoa and salt into a dough. Wrap and refrigerate for 1 hour, until elastic and smooth.

320 min

Shape the shells

Roll the dough out thin, 2–3mm. Cut into 10–11cm circles. Wrap each around a cannoli tube and seal the edge with egg white — press the seam firmly, or it will come apart while frying.

420 min

Fry the shells

Heat the frying oil to 180°C. Fry the cannoli on their tubes for 3–4 minutes, until golden. Lift out of the oil and immediately pull the tube free using a cloth — take care, it’s hot — then let cool completely.

55 min

Make the cream

Smooth the drained ricotta with a spatula. Mix in the powdered sugar and chocolate chips until smooth and even, then transfer to a piping bag.

65 min

Fill and serve

Just before serving, pipe the cream into the shells from both ends. Garnish the ends with candied citron and dust with powdered sugar. Serve immediately — filled too early, the shell turns soft.

About this dish

Cannoli are said to have originated in Sicilian convent pastry kitchens. The theory that they began at the Carta Nuova convent near Caltagirone is well known, and today they’re recognised around the world as one of the dishes that defines Italian cooking.

Draining the sheep’s-milk ricotta begins the day before. If it’s not drained well enough, moisture migrates into the shell the instant it’s filled, and the texture changes within minutes. That’s exactly why real pasticcerie in Sicily fill each shell to order. The contrast between a just-fried, crackling shell and cold, moist ricotta is a contrast that disappears as time passes.

Anyone who’s eaten one filled to order can never quite think of the pre-filled, shelf-stocked versions as the same thing as a “cannolo” again. That difference in experience is exactly the line between the real thing and the commercial product.

Chef Authen’s tip

"Leave the gun, take the cannoli" — the line from The Godfather (1972) — is arguably why the whole world knows the name today. It went on to become a symbol of Italian-American food culture far beyond Sicily. The nuns of Carta Nuova convent almost certainly never imagined their pastry would become world-famous through a mafia film’s punchline. One thing hasn’t changed, though: it’s still filled fresh, to order.
