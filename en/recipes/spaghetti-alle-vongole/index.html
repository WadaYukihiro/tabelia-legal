Authentic Spaghetti alle Vongole

Difficulty★★☆☆☆ Prep4 min Cook19 min Rest2 hr servings2

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (2 servings)

  • Clams (vongole)500 g
  • Spaghetti200 g
  • Garlic3 cloves
  • Extra virgin olive oil60 ml
  • Dry white wine100 ml
  • Flat-leaf parsley1 bunch
  • Chili pepper1

Method

12 hr

Purge the clams

Cover 500g clams with foil and purge in a dark place for 2 hours. Spread them in a single layer in a tray and cover with 3% brine (30g salt per litre of water) until submerged.

24 min

Boil the water and prep the aromatics

Bring a large pot of water to a boil, salted with 10g salt per litre. Drain the purged clams into a colander, scrub the shells together under running water and drain. Discard any broken clams or ones that stay open when tapped. Finely chop the parsley and crush the garlic with the flat of a knife.

33 min

Cook the aromatics

Heat 60ml olive oil with the crushed garlic and chilli over low heat until fragrant, about 3 minutes, until the garlic is just lightly coloured.

45 min

Open the clams

Add the clams to the pan, raise the heat to medium, pour in 100ml white wine and cover. Remove the clams once all have opened, 3–5 minutes, and set the liquid aside.

58 min

Cook the spaghetti

Add the spaghetti to the boiling salted water and cook 2 minutes short of the package time. Reserve 100ml pasta water.

62 min

Emulsify with the pasta

Transfer the pasta to the pan with the reserved clam liquid and pasta water. Toss vigorously over high heat for 1–2 minutes until the sauce clings glossily to the pasta.

71 min

Finish with parsley

Return the clams to the pan, scatter with the chopped parsley and serve.

About this dish

Fishermen in the Gulf of Naples land clams at dawn, and by that same morning they’re already in pasta — Spaghetti alle Vongole gets the Mediterranean’s bounty to the table by the shortest possible route.

Along the Campania coast, the “vongole veraci,” made quickly with clams gathered from the local sandy shores, is still the standard approach. White wine, garlic, parsley and olive oil — with just those few elements combined, the clams’ own flavour is drawn out into the sauce.

There are two versions, bianco (white) and rosso (red, with tomato), but in Naples bianco is by far the mainstream. Plenty of cooks argue that adding tomato buries the clam’s delicate flavour.

Chef Authen’s tip

Purging clams properly means 3% brine — 30g salt per litre of water — in a dark place for 2 hours. Fresh water or brine that’s too concentrated and the sand won’t come out. The clams are still alive at this point. Give them the right conditions and they’ll do their part. Anyone who serves a pasta with sand still in it seems to have forgotten that.
