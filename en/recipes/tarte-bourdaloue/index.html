Tarte BourdaloueBourdaloue Pear and Almond Tart

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep55 min Cook50 min Rest1 hr 20 min servings4

A pear tart said to originate from a pastry shop on Paris’s Rue Bourdaloue. Pears poached in vanilla syrup are nestled in a butter-and-ground-almond cream and baked. Judging both the tenderness of the pears and the baking of the almond cream determines the result of this Parisian pâtisserie classic.

Method

110 min

Make the sweet pastry

  • 1 Cream 90 g softened butter with 50 g icing sugar in a bowl.
  • 2 Add 1 egg and mix well.
  • 3 Sift in 180 g flour and 1 pinch of salt and cut through with a spatula until no dry flour remains. Continue once the dough holds together.
230 min

Rest the pastry

  • 1 Flatten the dough into a disc, wrap in cling film and refrigerate for 30 minutes.
310 min

Prepare the pears and vanilla

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile the pastry rests, peel 4 pears, halve lengthwise and scoop out the cores.
  • 2 Split the 1/2 vanilla pod for poaching lengthwise and scrape out the seeds.
45 min

Make the poaching syrup

  • 1 Heat 500 ml water, the 100 g sugar for poaching, and the vanilla seeds and pod in a saucepan over medium heat. Add the pears once the sugar dissolves.
515 min

Poach the pears

  • 1 Simmer gently over low heat for 12–15 minutes.
  • 2 Once a skewer slides in easily, turn off the heat and leave the pears to cool in the syrup.
620 min

Cool in the syrup

  • 1 Let the initial heat subside while the pears remain in the syrup.
78 min

Line the tart tin

  • 1 Roll the pastry on a floured surface into a circle about 24 cm across and 3 mm thick, then line the tart tin.
  • 2 Prick the base with a fork.
815 min

Rest the lined pastry

  • 1 Refrigerate for 15 minutes.
93 min

Preheat to 180°C

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile the pastry chills, begin preheating the oven to 180°C.
1010 min

Make the almond cream

  • 1 Beat 100 g room-temperature butter and 100 g caster sugar in a bowl until pale and fluffy.
  • 2 Add 2 eggs, 1 at a time, mixing well after each.
  • 3 Sift in 100 g ground almonds and 15 g flour, mix, then add 10 ml rum and stir until smooth.
115 min

Arrange the cream and pears

  • 1 Spread the almond cream evenly over the chilled pastry base.
  • 2 Lift the pears from the syrup and pat lightly dry, thinly slice across and fan them slightly as you arrange them radially.
1235 min

Bake the tart

  • 1 Bake at 180°C for 30–35 minutes.
  • 2 It is baked when the almond cream has risen and turned deep golden and a skewer inserted in the centre comes out without batter.
135 min

Glaze with jam

  • 1 Warm 30 g apricot jam in a small pan to loosen it and brush a thin glossy layer over the entire tart.
  • 2 Scatter 15 g flaked almonds around the edge.
1415 min

Cool slightly and slice

  • 1 Let the initial heat subside before slicing.

About this dish

A pastry shop on Rue Bourdaloue, near the Panthéon in Paris’s 5th arrondissement, is said to have been known for its pear tart in the 19th century. The combination of pears and almond cream came to be called tarte Bourdaloue after that shop.

The structure combines two techniques: gently poaching pears in vanilla-scented syrup and baking a crème d’amande made with butter, eggs and ground almonds. It is said to have been established among Parisian pastry cooks in the 19th century.

The tart is a fixture in Parisian pâtisserie windows from autumn into winter, when pears are plentiful. Bistros also include it on dessert menus, serving it warm or chilled after a meal.

Chef Authen’s tip

Several Parisian pastries bear the name Bourdaloue. Some accounts link them to the 17th-century preacher Louis Bourdaloue, others to the street. While the origin remains uncertain, the pairing of pears and almond cream has endured under the name. The history may be hazy, but the formula has never wavered. Parisian pastry memories seem to lie in combinations rather than names.
