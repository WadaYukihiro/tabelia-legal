Salade PérigourdinePérigord Duck and Walnut Salad

Difficulty★★☆☆☆ Prep45 min Cook40 min servings2

A traditional southwestern French starter bringing together Périgord’s three treasures—confit duck gizzards, smoked duck breast and foie gras—with walnut-oil vinaigrette. Warm potatoes cooked in duck fat, pommes de terre sarladaises, contrast with cold leaves and warm gizzards. Soft-boiled eggs let their yolks become part of the sauce. Walnut oil is never heated: emulsify with sherry vinegar and mustard and dress at the end to preserve the local walnut aroma.

Method

115 min

Crisp the lettuce and prepare the vegetables

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the leaves soak) remove the stalk from 1 tomato, about 150g, and cut into 6 wedges.
  • 2 Meanwhile(While the leaves soak) mince 1 shallot, about 20g, extremely finely.
  • 3 Meanwhile(While the leaves soak) mince 1 garlic clove and peel and halve the other 1 lengthways.
  • 4 Meanwhile(While the leaves soak) pick and coarsely chop 5g parsley leaves.
  • 5 Meanwhile(While the lettuce soaks) start boiling 1L water over high heat for the eggs.
  • 6 Core 150g Batavia lettuce and tear leaves into bite-sized pieces.
  • 7 Soak the torn leaves in a bowl of iced water for 10 minutes.
  • 8 Spin dry, then press between clean cloths to dry completely; refrigerate in the bowl.

Ready when

Proceed when the leaves are crisp and no water drips when squeezed.

27 min

Toast the walnuts

  • 1 Warm a dry frying pan over medium heat and spread in 40g walnut halves.
  • 2 Toast for 4–5 minutes, shaking constantly; remove to a plate and cool.
  • 3 Once slightly cooled, break by hand into 2–3 pieces each.

Ready when

They are ready when fragrant and the broken interiors are lightly coloured.

38 min

Emulsify the walnut vinaigrette

  • 1 Whisk 15ml sherry vinegar, 5g Dijon mustard, 2g salt and 0.3g black pepper in a small bowl for 30 seconds until the salt dissolves.
  • 2 Add 20g peeled, finely minced shallot and leave for 5 minutes.
  • 3 Whisk constantly while slowly trickling in 45ml walnut oil to make vinaigrette, an emulsion of vinegar and oil.

Ready when

It is ready when thickened and cloudy, with no oil layer appearing when you stop whisking.

412 min

Soft-boil the eggs with flowing yolks

  • 1 Return the prepared 1L water to the boil.
  • 2 Lower 2 room-temperature eggs gently with a ladle and boil for 6 minutes over medium-high heat, maintaining the boil.
  • 3 Cool in iced water for 2 minutes, crack the whole shell and peel under water.

Ready when

They are ready when the whites are firm and the yolks feel slightly mobile inside when held.

58 min

Top garlic toast with foie gras mi-cuit

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the bread toasts) trim 80g room-temperature smoked duck breast to leave 2mm fat and slice very thinly, 1.5mm.
  • 2 Toast 2 slices country bread, 1cm thick, in a pan or toaster for 2–3 minutes until pale golden on both sides.
  • 3 While hot, rub one entire side with the cut face of 1 halved garlic clove.
  • 4 Dip a knife blade in boiling water for 10 seconds and wipe; take 80g foie gras mi-cuit, cooked chilled foie gras, from the refrigerator and slice 1cm thick.
  • 5 Cool the toast for 1–2 minutes, add the foie gras and sprinkle with 1g fleur de sel and 0.3g black pepper.

Ready when

It is ready when the foie gras keeps sharp edges and has a slight sheen on the toast.

Caution

Hot toast melts foie gras easily.

630 min

Cook the Sarlat-style potatoes in duck fat

  • 1 Peel 250g potatoes, slice into 3mm rounds and dry with a cloth without soaking.
  • 2 Melt 45g duck fat in a pan about 28cm across and arrange potatoes with as little overlap as possible.
  • 3 Cover and cook over medium-low heat for 10 minutes, then uncover and cook for 10–15 minutes, turning.
  • 4 Sprinkle with 3g salt and 0.4g black pepper, add 1 minced garlic clove and cook together for 1 minute.
  • 5 Add half the chopped parsley, 2.5g, and mix; remove only the potatoes to a warm place, leaving the duck fat in the pan.

Ready when

They are ready when golden on both sides, crisp at the edges and easily pierced at the centre.

Caution

Moving them too much early prevents their surfaces setting and makes them break.

76 min

Reheat and brown the confit gizzards

  • 1 Lightly wipe surface fat from 120g confit duck gizzards, gizzards slowly cooked in duck fat, and slice 5mm thick across the fibres.
  • 2 Warm the remaining duck fat in the potato pan over medium heat; spread in the gizzards and cook for 2–3 minutes.
  • 3 Turn once, cook 30 seconds, remove to a plate and cover loosely with foil to keep warm.

Ready when

They are ready when the cut edges brown, the fat sizzles and the centres are warm.

88 min

Assemble cold leaves with warm duck and potatoes

  • 1 Transfer the chilled lettuce to a large bowl, drizzle with 2/3 of the vinaigrette and toss gently from underneath by hand for 15 seconds.
  • 2 Pile the dressed leaves high on 2 plates.
  • 3 Arrange 3 tomato wedges around each and lay the thin smoked duck slices in light folds over the leaves.
  • 4 Arrange warm potatoes along the leaf edges and scatter warm gizzards on top.
  • 5 Halve the soft-boiled eggs lengthways and put 1 egg on each plate; divide 1g fleur de sel over the cut surfaces.
  • 6 Scatter the toasted walnuts and drizzle remaining vinaigrette over the leaves and gizzards.
  • 7 Scatter the remaining 2.5g parsley and add foie gras toast at the plate edge.

Ready when

The salad is ready when all the leaves are glossy without vinaigrette pooling beneath.

About this dish

Thinking of Périgord brings duck, foie gras and walnuts to the table. Salade périgourdine offers these local flavours together on one plate.

For an autumn lunch it can form the centre of the meal with bread. Ingredients vary by household and restaurant; this version combines gizzards, smoked duck breast and chilled foie gras.

Chef Authen’s tip

Périgourdine means Périgord-style. Though called a salad, it contains duck and walnuts as well as leaves, giving it the presence of a full course.
