Saint-Honoré

Difficulty★★★★★ Prep1 hr 45 min Cook45 min Rest1 hr 50 min servings4

Named for the patron saint of bakers and pastry chefs, this dessert is said to have originated at Paris's Chiboust pastry shop in the 19th century. Small choux puffs are fixed with caramel in a close ring around puff pastry, with crème Chiboust, a light blend of pastry cream and Italian meringue, piped into the center. Combining 4 techniques—puff pastry, choux, caramel and cream—it is considered a technical landmark of French pâtisserie. Store-bought all-butter puff pastry makes the base reproducible at home.

About this dish

Rue Saint-Honoré, near the Louvre in central Paris, is named after the patron saint of bakers and pastry chefs. The Chiboust pastry shop on this street is said to have invented the dessert in the mid-19th century.

Its construction—small choux fixed with caramel tightly around a puff-pastry base, with crème Chiboust, pastry cream lightened with Italian meringue, piped into the center—brought together the finest skills of Parisian pastry chefs at the time.

Large versions are sometimes made for special celebrations such as weddings and birthdays. Many renowned Paris pâtisseries still feature Saint-Honoré as a signature attraction. Each shop shows its individuality in the piping and size of the choux, in a dessert regarded as a technical landmark of French pastry.

Chef Authen’s tip

Crème Chiboust takes its name directly from the pastry shop that devised Saint-Honoré. Italian meringue makes it lighter than ordinary pastry cream while allowing it to hold its shape. The shop disappeared within the 19th century, but its name survived as a technique. This is one form of immortality in pastry.
