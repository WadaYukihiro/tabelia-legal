Tarte au Sucre du NordNorthern French Sugar Tart

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep50 min Cook25 min Rest2 hr 20 min servings4

A brioche-dough sweet from Hauts-de-France in northern France, said to have originated as a way to use leftover bakery dough. Finger-made hollows are filled with vergeoise beet sugar and crème fraîche, then baked to a caramelized surface. Judging the proofing of the yeast dough and the color of the topping determines the result. A staple made in homes and bakeries for Sunday breakfast or an afternoon goûter.

Method

13 min

Dissolve the yeast in milk

  • 1 Warm 80ml milk to about 35°C and pour into a bowl.
  • 2 Crumble in 10g fresh yeast and dissolve, then dissolve in 5g granulated sugar for activating the yeast.
210 min

Activate the yeast

  • 1 Leave at room temperature for 10 minutes.
  • 2 Small bubbles rising on the surface indicate that the yeast is active.
310 min

Mix and knead the dough

  • 1 Mix 250g bread flour and 4g salt in a bowl.
  • 2 Make a well in the center, add 2 eggs, 25g granulated sugar for the dough and the activated milk mixture, and mix until a dough forms.
  • 3 Turn onto the worktop and knead for about 10 minutes until the surface is smooth.
48 min

Knead in the butter

  • 1 Add 60g softened butter in 3 additions, kneading thoroughly after each.

Ready when

Continue when the dough no longer sticks to your hands and its surface becomes glossy.

Caution

Bring the butter to room temperature and soften before adding. Cold butter will not mix evenly into the dough.

51 hr 30 min

Let the dough rise

  • 1 Shape into a ball, return to the bowl, cover with plastic wrap and leave in a warm place, around 27–30°C.

Ready when

The first rise is complete when the dough grows to 2 times its original size after 1.5 hours.

610 min

Line the pan with dough

  • 1 Lightly butter the inside of a 22cm tart pan, using additional butter.
  • 2 Gently deflate the risen dough and press evenly over the bottom of the pan.
  • 3 Make evenly spaced indentations all over the surface with your fingertips.
740 min

Proof the dough again

  • 1 Cover the pan with a cloth and proof in a warm place for 40 minutes, until the dough has visibly expanded.
810 min

Make the vergeoise paste

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile waiting for the second rise, start preheating the oven to 190°C.
  • 2 Mix 100g vergeoise and 80g crème fraîche in a bowl to form a smooth paste.
  • 3 Cut the 40g butter for the topping into 1cm cubes.
95 min

Add the topping

  • 1 Brush the edge of the risen dough with 1 egg yolk.
  • 2 Spoon the vergeoise paste into the indentations and spread it over the entire surface.
  • 3 Distribute the diced butter evenly.
1022 min

Bake the tart

  • 1 Bake at 190°C for 20–25 minutes.
  • 2 The tart is done when the dough is deep golden throughout and the topping bubbles and caramelizes.
115 min

Cool briefly and slice

  • 1 Leave in the pan for about 5 minutes to settle, then slice and serve warm.

About this dish

Hauts-de-France, bordering Belgium, once prospered through coal mining and the textile industry. In this region, bakery windows display tarte au sucre, a brioche-dough tart baked with sugar, throughout the year.

It is said to have begun as a way to avoid wasting dough left over after baking bread. The method of making hollows with the fingers and filling them with vergeoise beet sugar and crème fraîche transformed a humble leftover sweet into an emblem of the region.

It is traditionally eaten with coffee or hot chocolate at Sunday breakfast or an afternoon goûter. Recipes vary slightly from household to household, and local bakeries often treat it as a signature product.

Chef Authen’s tip

Vergeoise is a beet sugar distinctive to northern France and Belgium. Repeated secondary boiling during refining gives it a particular depth and gentle caramel aroma that define the sweetness of this region’s pastries. In Hauts-de-France bakeries, bags of this sugar still sit naturally alongside the flour. A region’s sweetness begins with its choice of sugar.
