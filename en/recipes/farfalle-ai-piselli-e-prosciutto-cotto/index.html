About this dish

In Emilia-Romagna, spring moves from the season of flowering rapeseed greens to that of legumes. Peas can be harvested only for a short period in early summer, and farming families combined them straight from the garden with pasta.

Prosciutto cotto, cooked cured ham, corresponds to the ham familiar in Japan, but Italian versions retain pork’s natural sweetness. In a simple butter-and-Parmigiano sauce, the ham’s saltiness and the peas’ sweetness echo each other. During the limited April-to-June season for fresh peas, this dish frequently appears on Emilian family tables.

Chef Authen’s tip

In most areas, fresh peas are sold for less than 2 months of the year. Emilian farmers have a long history of treating peas as a spring ingredient and judging the dish by their just-picked sweetness. Frozen peas can be used, but then it is more accurate to distinguish the result from a dish made with seasonal ingredients.
