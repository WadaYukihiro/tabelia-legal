Risotto al BaroloBarolo Risotto

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep7 min Cook30 min servings4

A luxurious Piedmontese risotto made with a generous amount of Barolo, the “king of wines”. Its deep crimson colour is striking, and the wine’s astringency combines with butter and Parmigiano fat to create complex savour. A dish for special occasions.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Carnaroli rice320 g
  • Barolo red wineUse high quality even for cooking.400 ml
  • Beef brothKeep warm.1000 ml
  • Golden onionFinely chopped.1 piece
  • ButterReserve half cold for the mantecatura.80 g
  • Parmigiano Reggiano60 g

Method

110 min

Cook the onion

  • 1 Melt 40g butter over medium heat in a pot.
  • 2 Add 1 finely chopped onion and cook over low heat for 10 minutes until translucent.

Ready when

The onion is translucent and softened.

Caution

Burning creates bitterness; cook patiently over low heat.

27 min

Toast the rice and add Barolo

  • 1 Add 320g Carnaroli and toast over medium heat for 2 minutes.
  • 2 Pour in 400ml Barolo all at once and stir while cooking off the alcohol, about 5 minutes.

Ready when

The alcohol aroma has gone and the wine colour has passed into the rice.

Caution

Thorough toasting firms the grain surface and makes starch less likely to dissolve.

318 min

Cook with gradual broth additions

  • 1 Add 1 ladle warm beef broth and stir until absorbed.
  • 2 Add the next 1 ladle once absorbed; repeat for 15–18 minutes.

Ready when

The rice is al dente.

Caution

Always keep the broth warm; cold broth stops the rice cooking.

42 min

Mantecare

  • 1 Turn off the heat, add 40g cold butter and 60g Parmigiano and stir vigorously.

Ready when

The butter and cheese have emulsified into a creamy risotto.

Caution

Use cold butter; warm butter emulsifies less easily.

52 min

Rest and serve

  • 1 Cover and rest for 1–2 minutes.
  • 2 Plate and serve.

Ready when

The risotto has rested for 1–2 minutes and its temperature has evened out.

About this dish

Barolo is called the king of wines. Made from Nebbiolo, this red wine reaches the market after at least 3–5 years of ageing. Pouring an entire bottle costing from several thousand to tens of thousands of yen into a risotto without restraint reflects the wealth and convictions of the Langhe hills.

Watching the risotto turn purple is an experience of culinary transformation. Vialone Nano rice absorbs Barolo’s pigment, gradually becoming a deep red-purple. This visual beauty is among the cooking phenomena hardest to convey in a photograph.

Replacing Barolo with cheaper wine is technically possible, but Langhe cooks would not call the result Barolo risotto. Cooking from a wine region means the local wine itself is an ingredient.

Chef Authen’s tip

Using a whole 750ml bottle of Barolo for risotto is the authentic Langhe recipe. The dish is completed when Barolo’s tannins and complexity emulsify with the rice starch. Risotto coloured purple with cheap red wine might instead be called “purple risotto”. What would a Langhe cook think of someone who can drink Barolo in its homeland but begrudges a bottle for risotto?
