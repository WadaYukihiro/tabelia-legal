Pesce all'Acqua PazzaFish in Acqua Pazza

Difficulty★★☆☆☆ Prep10 min Cook20 min servings2

A Neapolitan fishermen’s dish of white fish gently braised in “crazy water” with cherry tomatoes, garlic and white wine. The broth is a precious concentration of flavour. The key is to avoid overcooking and finish before the fish turns white and firm.

Method

14 min

Cut the tomatoes and parsley

  • 1 Halve 200g cherry tomatoes.
  • 2 Finely chop the flat-leaf parsley.
24 min

Cook the base

  • 1 Put 50ml olive oil and crushed garlic in a frying pan over medium heat.
  • 2 When fragrant, add the tomatoes and olives and sauté for 2 minutes.

Ready when

Proceed when the tomatoes begin to break down slightly.

35 min

Add the fish

  • 1 Add 100ml white wine and 150ml water and bring to the boil.
  • 2 Add the fish and cook over medium-high heat for 4 minutes, spooning the liquid over it.

Ready when

Proceed to the next step after 4 minutes.

410 min

Braise gently

  • 1 Carefully turn the fish, cover and simmer over low heat for 8–10 minutes.

Ready when

It is ready when the flesh turns white and feels as though it releases from the base of the bones.

Caution

Overcooking toughens the flesh.

52 min

Serve in deep plates with the broth

  • 1 Scatter with parsley and serve in deep plates with the broth.

About this dish

Acqua pazza means “crazy water”. The leading explanation for its name traces it to fishermen offshore cooking fish directly in seawater, “crazy” because of its high salt content. A fishermen’s meal of seawater, tomatoes and wild herbs eaten on deck is considered its prototype.

Today, fresh white fish is gently braised at low temperature with garlic, cherry tomatoes, parsley, olive oil, white wine and water. It lies somewhere between soup and braised fish. The fish’s flavour dissolves into the liquid while the liquid’s flavour penetrates the fish.

Neapolitan fishermen say, “Do not interfere with fresh fish.” This method, handed down among them, puts the freshness of the ingredients first.

Chef Authen’s tip

The Neapolitan custom is always to eat the acqua pazza broth by dipping bread into it, never discarding it. Leaving the broth is considered equivalent to throwing away half the dish. If you have the bowl cleared without dipping anything into it, consider where the “pazza”—the madness—really lies.
