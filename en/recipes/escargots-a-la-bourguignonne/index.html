Ingredients (4 servings)

100Authenticity
  • Canned Burgundy snails (Helix pomatia)24 snailsPrecooked in liquid; check the label for Helix pomatia. Rinse in cold water to remove the packing liquid
  • Unsalted butter170 gLeave at room temperature for 60 minutes to soften
  • Shallots17 gMince very finely
  • Garlic5 g1 clove; crush and work into a paste with a knife
  • Parsley leaves12 gFinely chop the leaves only. Use the leaves from a fresh flat-leaf parsley bunch, removing the stems before weighing.
  • Salt4 g
  • Ground black pepper1 g
  • Fine dried plain breadcrumbs5 gSprinkle lightly over the butter before baking. Use fine dry plain breadcrumbs; grind or sieve coarser crumbs before weighing.

Method

11 hr

Soften the butter

  • 1 Put 170g unsalted butter in a bowl and leave at room temperature for 60 minutes.

Ready when

Continue when the butter yields readily to pressure from a flexible spatula.

210 min

Finely chop the shallot, garlic and parsley

  • 1 Mince 17g shallot very finely.
  • 2 Crush 5g garlic with the flat of a knife, then chop and work it into a paste with the blade.
  • 3 Finely chop 12g parsley leaves.

Ready when

Continue when all the aromatics are finely chopped.

320 min

Mix and chill the snail butter

  • 1 Add the chopped shallot, garlic and parsley, 4g salt and 1g black pepper to the softened butter. Work with a flexible spatula until uniformly green to make beurre d’escargot, a garlic-parsley compound butter.
  • 2 Refrigerate for 15 minutes until scoopable but firm enough to hold its shape.
  • 3 MeanwhileWhile the butter chills, preheat the oven to 240°C.

Ready when

The mixing is complete when parsley is distributed throughout and no streaks of white butter remain.

Continue when a spoonful of butter holds its shape.

410 min

Rinse the snails, fill the dishes and seal with butter

  • 1 Drain 24 canned snails in a strainer, rinse off the packing liquid with cold water and pat dry with paper towels.
  • 2 Put 1/2 teaspoon snail butter in the bottom of each well in 4 snail dishes with 6 wells each.
  • 3 Put 1 snail in each well and use the back of a spoon to press the remaining butter around it, sealing the opening.
  • 4 Lightly sprinkle 5g breadcrumbs over the butter.

Ready when

Bake when every well is filled level with butter and the snails are completely covered.

Caution

If the openings are not sealed with butter, the butter will run out during baking and the snails will dry out.

58 min

Bake at 240°C until bubbling and serve hot

  • 1 Arrange the snail dishes on a baking sheet and bake on the upper rack at 240°C for 7–8 minutes.
  • 2 Remove the dishes with oven mitts and serve immediately.

Ready when

They are ready when the butter is melted and bubbling around the edges of the wells and the breadcrumbs are lightly browned.

Caution

Canned snails are already cooked; overbaking makes them tough.

About this dish

In France, snails are eaten most often during the year-end festivities; around two-thirds of annual consumption is said to occur at Christmas. Burgundy-style snails, sealed beneath green garlic-parsley butter, appear on festive tables at this time.

The large snail named escargot de Bourgogne grows slowly and is not farmed in France. Wild populations declined through overharvesting. A 1979 ministerial order banned collecting those with shells under 3cm in diameter year-round, and larger ones from April through June.

French canners use wild Burgundy snails collected in the northern Balkans. Increasingly, they arrive already cooked in the country where they were gathered. The industry code permits the name “escargots de Bourgogne” only on cans containing this species alone.

Chef Authen’s tip

In 2022, a research institute in Matsusaka, Japan, announced that it had achieved a complete breeding cycle for Burgundy snails, and canned snails from its facility went on sale. If the announcement is accurate, a facility raising this species from eggs is located in Matsusaka rather than in its namesake region of France.
