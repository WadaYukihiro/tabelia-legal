Gâteau BasqueBasque Cake with Pastry Cream

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep50 min Cook40 min Rest2 hr 10 min servings4

A cake from the French Basque Country, enclosing pastry cream in buttery shortbread-like dough, scored with a lattice and baked. Its appeal is the contrast between fragrant thick pastry and the custard within. Enclosing the cream without breaking the soft dough is the main challenge; thorough chilling is the key. Handed down in homes and pastry shops, it is a point of regional pride.

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Cake flour (for pastry)250 g
  • Baking powderGives a slight rise and a texture between shortbread and cake4 g
  • Unsalted butter (for pastry)Soften at room temperature140 g
  • Granulated sugar (for pastry)130 g
  • Egg (for pastry)Binds the dough1 piece
  • Salt (for pastry)2 g
  • Zest of an unwaxed lemon grown in JapanGrate into the dough1 piece
  • Whole milk (for the cream)250 ml
  • Egg yolks (for the cream)3 pieces
  • Granulated sugar (for the cream)60 g
  • CornstarchThickens the cream25 g
  • Dark rumFlavors the cream; a traditional Basque formula10 ml

Method

112 min

Make the dough

  • 1 Cream 140 g softened pastry butter and 130 g pastry sugar in a bowl until pale.
  • 2 Mix in 1 pastry egg and the grated zest of 1/2 lemon.
  • 3 Sift in 250 g cake flour, 4 g baking powder and 2 g salt together; fold with a rubber spatula until no dry flour remains. Proceed when the soft dough forms a single mass.
21 hr

Chill and rest the dough

  • 1 Divide the dough in a 2:1 ratio, flatten each piece into a disc, wrap and refrigerate thoroughly for 1 hour.

Ready when

This dough is soft and cannot be shaped unless well chilled.

315 min

Make the pastry cream

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the dough chills) make the crème pâtissière, or pastry cream.
  • 2 Heat 250 ml milk in a saucepan over medium heat until just below boiling, then turn off.
  • 3 In a bowl, mix 3 cream egg yolks with 60 g cream sugar until pale, then sift in 25 g cornstarch and mix.
  • 4 Gradually add the warm milk while mixing, return to the saucepan and cook over medium heat. Stir constantly; after it thickens and bubbles, beat for 1 minute, turn off the heat and mix in 10 ml rum.
430 min

Chill and set the cream

  • 1 Spread the cream on a tray, cover with plastic wrap directly against the surface and refrigerate for 30 minutes.
510 min

Line the tin and fill with cream

  • 1 Start preheating the oven to 180°C.
  • 2 Meanwhile(While the oven heats) lightly butter the inside of an 18 cm tin with extra butter not included in the quantities.
  • 3 Roll the larger dough portion on a floured worktop to about 24 cm in diameter and line the base and sides of the tin.
  • 4 Loosen the cold cream with a rubber spatula, pour in and level the surface.
68 min

Cover and score a lattice

  • 1 Roll the smaller portion to a circle about 18 cm in diameter and lay over the cream, pressing the edges with your fingers to join to the lower dough.
  • 2 Beat 1 glazing egg yolk, brush over the surface and score a diagonal lattice with the back of a fork.
740 min

Bake the Basque cake

  • 1 Bake at 180°C for 35–40 minutes.
  • 2 It is ready when the whole surface is deep golden and the lattice stands out clearly.
840 min

Cool completely in the tin

  • 1 Cool completely in the tin.
  • 2 Cooling firms the pastry so that the cream layer shows neatly when sliced.

About this dish

The Basque Country in southwestern France, bordering Spain, has preserved its own language and culture. Gâteau basque is said to derive from a cake sold in the 19th century by pastry maker Marianne Hirigoyen in the spa town of Cambo-les-Bains.

Inside its thick butter pastry, the filling follows 2 schools: custard or black cherry jam from the local village of Itxassou. Some say coastal preferences favor cream and mountain preferences cherry; household and village styles remain alive today.

It is now a signature sweet found in every Basque pastry shop, and a gâteau basque festival takes place in Cambo-les-Bains in October. The surface lattice or traditional Basque spiral motif also marks it as a symbol of the region.

Chef Authen’s tip

Alongside lattices, gâteau basque surfaces sometimes bear a lauburu, the traditional Basque swirling cross. The cake’s surface becomes a place for the region’s emblem. Debates about cream or cherry filling happen because this cake is not merely a sweet, but the memory of Basque households themselves.
