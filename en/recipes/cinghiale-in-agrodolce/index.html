Cinghiale in AgrodolceSweet-and-Sour Wild Boar

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep55 min Cook5 hr 5 min Rest24 hr servings2

A Maremma hunting dish: wild boar is marinated for a full day and night in red wine with aromatic vegetables and juniper to remove strong odors, then slowly braised. To finish, agrodolce made by adding red wine vinegar to caramelized sugar is combined with pine nuts, raisins, high-cocoa chocolate and orange peel, enveloping the gamey meat in sweetness, acidity and bitterness. Reducing and straining the marinade, controlling the caramel’s browning and finally binding the sauce with chocolate determine the flavor.

Method

125 min

Cut the boar and immerse in a red-wine marinade

  • 1 Trim thick silverskin and excess connective membrane from 600 g wild boar shoulder and cut into 4–5 cm cubes.
  • 2 Slice 75 g onion 2 mm thick, cut 50 g carrot into 5 mm rounds and remove the strings from 40 g celery before cutting into 3 cm chunks.
  • 3 Peel 1 garlic clove, crush with the flat of a knife and remove the central germ.
  • 4 Lightly crush 8 juniper berries and 10 black peppercorns with the flat of a knife to release their aromas.
  • 5 Put the boar, cut vegetables, crushed garlic, juniper, peppercorns, 2 bay leaves and 2 cloves in a glass or enamel container.
  • 6 Pour in 500 ml red wine and 40 ml red wine vinegar, press the meat down by hand and apply plastic wrap directly against the surface.

Ready when

All the meat should be submerged in the wine mixture, with no surfaces exposed to air.

Caution

Do not marinate in aluminum containers, which are vulnerable to acid.

224 hr

Marinate for 24 hours to color and scent the meat throughout

  • 1 Put the container in the refrigerator at around 4°C for 24 hours.
  • 2 Every 6 hours, turn the meat 1 time with clean tongs and replace the plastic wrap against the surface.

Ready when

The meat should be reddish purple on its cut faces as well as its outer surface, with a strong aroma of juniper and cloves rising from the liquid.

Caution

Do not marinate raw meat at room temperature.

330 min

Reduce and clarify the marinade for the braising base

  • 1 Strain through a colander to separate the meat and aromatics from the liquid. Discard the marinating vegetables and spices, which have fulfilled their role.
  • 2 Carefully pat the boar dry with paper towels, arrange on a tray, cover and refrigerate until just before searing.
  • 3 Transfer the strained marinade to a small saucepan and bring to the boil over medium heat.
  • 4 Skim off and discard the gray scum and cloudy protein with a ladle 5–6 times.
  • 5 Reduce the heat to medium-low and simmer for 15–20 minutes, reducing by half to about 250 ml.
  • 6 Strain the reduced liquid through a fine sieve lined with paper towels and reserve.

Ready when

The volume should be about 250 ml, the bubbles fine and glossy, and the cloudiness gone.

430 min

Prepare the sauce additions and aromatic vegetables

  • 1 Put 40 g raisins in a small bowl, pour over 50 ml Vin Santo and soak for 30 minutes.
  • 2 Spread 30 g pine nuts in a dry frying pan and toast over low heat for 3–4 minutes, shaking constantly; transfer to a plate to cool when lightly golden.
  • 3 Cut 20 g candied orange peel into 3 mm dice.
  • 4 Finely chop 20 g dark chocolate (70% cocoa) and place in a bowl.
  • 5 For the braise, cut 75 g onion, 50 g carrot and 40 g celery into uniform 5 mm dice; crush 1 garlic clove and remove the germ.

Ready when

The raisins should be plump and spring back when pressed.

The pine nuts should be lightly colored to the center and release a toasted, oily aroma.

520 min

Flour the boar and sear deeply on all sides

  • 1 Remove the boar from the refrigerator and season evenly with 4 g salt.
  • 2 Spread 20 g cake flour on a tray, roll the meat in it to coat lightly and tap off all excess flour by hand (infarinatura means lightly flouring before cooking).
  • 3 Heat 40 ml olive oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. When the oil shimmers and begins to smoke lightly, add just half the meat without overlapping.
  • 4 Sear each side undisturbed for 2–3 minutes until deep brown, about 8 minutes in total, then remove to a plate. Sear and remove the remaining half in the same way.

Ready when

All sides should be dark brown, with brown deposits, or fond, stuck to the base of the pot.

620 min

Cook pancetta and soffritto and mellow the tomato paste

  • 1 Cut 60 g pancetta into 5 mm-wide batons. Add 20 ml olive oil to the pot from which the meat was removed and fry the pancetta over medium-low heat for 5 minutes.
  • 2 Add the onion, carrot and celery cut into 5 mm dice and the 1 crushed garlic clove; sauté over medium heat for 12–15 minutes. Constantly scrape the browned base with a wooden spoon, dissolving the deposits in the vegetables’ moisture.
  • 3 Add 20 g tomato paste and fry over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, pressing it out against the sides with a wooden spoon.

Ready when

The vegetables’ edges should have softened and everything should be lightly golden; the paste should change from bright red to dark reddish brown and smell sweet.

710 min

Start the braise with reduced marinade and broth

  • 1 Return the seared boar and all its collected juices to the pot.
  • 2 Pour in 250 ml reduced marinade and boil over high heat for 2 minutes to remove the sharp smell of alcohol.
  • 3 Add 200 ml warmed meat broth (stock made with meat and aromatic vegetables), then tuck 1 bay leaf, 1 rosemary sprig and 4 sage leaves between the pieces of meat.
  • 4 Once boiling again, reduce to low heat and cover with the lid slightly ajar.

Ready when

The liquid should cover about 8/10 of the meat and simmer quietly, with a bubble rising every few seconds.

82 hr 30 min

Braise for 2 hours 30 minutes until the boar falls apart

  • 1 Meanwhile(During the final 30 minutes of braising) make the agrodolce caramel in a small saucepan (step 9).
  • 2 Braise over low heat for 2 hours 30 minutes.
  • 3 Open the lid every 30 minutes, gently turn the meat from the bottom with a wooden spoon and check for sticking.
  • 4 If the liquid falls below half the meat’s height, add the remaining warm broth in 50–100 ml additions, 200 ml in total, to maintain the level.
  • 5 Finally, use a thermometer to verify that the center of the meat has been heated to at least 75°C for at least 1 minute.

Ready when

A bamboo skewer should pass through the center without resistance, and light pressure from the edge of a wooden spoon should split the fibers.

The braising liquid should be thick and leave a glossy film on the pot’s sides.

912 min

Caramelize the sugar and add vinegar for agrodolce

  • 1 Meanwhile(Carry out this stage during the last 30 minutes of the boar’s braise.)
  • 2 Spread 30 g granulated sugar evenly in a small saucepan and set over medium heat.
  • 3 Once it begins melting at the edges, swirl the pan without stirring with a spoon, distributing the melted portion and caramelizing to a deep amber over 3–4 minutes.
  • 4 Turn off the heat and carefully pour in 60 ml red wine vinegar in 2–3 additions, holding the pan away from your face.
  • 5 Return to low heat and simmer for 1–2 minutes, dissolving the hardened caramel with a wooden spoon to form a thick syrup.

Ready when

All sugar lumps should dissolve into a uniform syrup, with the sharp sting of acidity replaced by a sweet-and-sour aroma.

Caution

Vinegar splashes violently when added to hot caramel; do not put your face or hands above the pan.

1025 min

Combine the agrodolce, raisins, pine nuts and peel and reduce

  • 1 Remove the 1 bay leaf, rosemary stem and sage leaves from the braising pot with cooking chopsticks.
  • 2 Spoon off excess fat floating on the surface.
  • 3 Pour in all the agrodolce syrup and shake the pot to mingle it with the braising liquid.
  • 4 Add the 40 g raisins together with their Vin Santo soaking liquid, the 30 g toasted pine nuts and 20 g orange peel cut into 3 mm dice.
  • 5 Cook uncovered over medium-low heat for 20–25 minutes, turning from the bottom with a wooden spoon every 5 minutes.

Ready when

The sauce should be thick enough to briefly retain a spoon track and have a dark sheen on its surface.

1110 min

Melt in the chocolate for richness and shine

  • 1 Turn off the heat and scatter 20 g chopped dark chocolate all over the sauce.
  • 2 Shake the pot and stir gently with a wooden spoon, melting the chocolate completely with residual heat.
  • 3 Stir in the remaining 2 g salt and 2 g freshly ground black pepper.
  • 4 Warm over very low heat for just 1 minute without boiling.

Ready when

No chocolate pieces should remain visible; the sauce should be glossy blackish brown, with sweetness, acidity and bitterness united.

Caution

Boiling after adding chocolate makes the sauce prone to separating.

1215 min

Rest and serve on warmed plates

  • 1 Cover, remove from the heat and rest for 10 minutes.
  • 2 Warm 2 deep plates with boiling water or in a 60°C oven.
  • 3 Divide the boar evenly between them and spoon the remaining sauce, with its pine nuts and raisins, over the meat to cover it.

Ready when

It is ready when the sauce clings to the meat in a glossy film rather than running off across the plate.

About this dish

Tuscan wild boar cooking includes the dolceforte tradition of accompanying the meat with a sweet-and-sour sauce. Layering fruit and sweet elements over a meat dish is a tradition distinct from modern braises centered on savory flavors.

At an autumn table, it becomes a main course to savor slowly after a small appetizer. Its rich combination is one to enjoy on a day spent lingering around the table with family or guests.

Chef Authen’s tip

Agrodolce is a word for sweet-and-sour flavor. Even when chocolate is not visible in the name, Tuscan dolceforte meat dishes can include cocoa.
