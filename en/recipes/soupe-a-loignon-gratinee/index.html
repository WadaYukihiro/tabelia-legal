Soupe à l'Oignon GratinéeFrench Onion Soup Gratinée

Difficulty★★☆☆☆ Prep15 min Cook1 hr 10 min servings4

A dish that warmed workers at Les Halles, Paris’s central market, late at night. Onions are caramelized for more than 40 minutes, then slowly simmered in beef broth. Baguette topped with Gruyère floats on the soup before it is baked in the oven. Layers of onion sweetness and toasted cheese make this a classic of Parisian home cooking.

Method

115 min

Prepare the onions and baguette

  • 1 Halve 1000 g onions lengthwise and slice 3 mm thick along the grain.
  • 2 Cut half a baguette diagonally into slices 1.5 cm thick.
  • 3 Grate the Gruyère.
210 min

Start cooking the onions in butter

  • 1 Melt 50 g butter in a heavy pan over medium heat, add the onions and sprinkle with a pinch of salt.

Caution

For the first 10 minutes, stir occasionally to soften without burning.

335 min

Caramelize the onions

  • 1 Add 1 tsp sugar, reduce to low heat and cook for at least 30 minutes, stirring thoroughly from the bottom every 5 minutes.
  • 2 If the bottom dries out and threatens to burn, add 2 tbsp water to dissolve the deposits and continue cooking.

Caution

Aim for deep amber onions with a thin layer of browned deposits, or fond, on the bottom of the pan.

44 min

Add flour and deglaze with white wine

  • 1 Sprinkle 15 g soft wheat flour over the onions to coat them and cook for 1–2 minutes until the flouriness disappears.
  • 2 Pour in 100 ml dry white wine, scrape the browned deposits from the bottom and simmer over medium heat for 2–3 minutes, reducing until the sharp smell of alcohol around the pan mellows.
56 min

Toast the baguette

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile the soup simmers, begin preheating the oven to 220°C.
  • 2 Arrange the baguette on a baking tray and toast without toppings for 2–3 minutes on each side, removing when the edges are golden.
620 min

Simmer with the broth

  • 1 Add 1200 ml beef broth, 3 thyme sprigs and 1 bay leaf. Bring to a boil over high heat, reduce to low and simmer for 20 minutes.
  • 2 Skim off any foam that rises to the surface as needed.
  • 3 Remove the thyme and bay leaf and season with salt and black pepper.

Ready when

The soup should have thickened and absorbed the sweetness of the onions.

710 min

Add cheese and bake

  • 1 Divide the soup among 4 ovenproof soup bowls and float 1–2 toasted baguette slices on each.
  • 2 Distribute 150 g Gruyère generously and evenly over the top and bake at 220°C for 8–10 minutes.
  • 3 Remove when the cheese has melted and bubbles dark golden around the edges.

About this dish

Les Halles, Paris’s central market, was once called the “stomach of Paris.” Wholesalers and porters passed through it as they worked through the night. Small eating houses nearby, open from late night until dawn, needed inexpensive, warming dishes, and this soup made with onions, bread and cheese became a staple.

Even today it appears on almost every winter bistro menu, enjoyed at lunch or dinner during cold weather. It has also become established as home cooking throughout France, with regional variations in the cheese and the way the broth is made.

Originally a practical dish to satisfy hungry night workers, it is now a classic bistro starter that gives visitors a taste of Paris. Then as now, it is served piping hot straight from the oven.

Chef Authen’s tip

Browning onions is often treated casually as simply frying them, but it is a slow chemical transformation in which sugars and amino acids react over nearly 40 minutes. The late-night eating houses of Les Halles could keep serving this soup cheaply because the pan largely carried on the work itself. Those 40 minutes are the fair price of that deep amber color.
