OpéraOpéra Cake

Thin almond sponge, biscuit Joconde, is soaked in coffee syrup and layered with coffee buttercream and ganache. “Opéra” is written on the chocolate glaze of this Parisian cake, whose total height should not exceed 3cm.

Ingredients (9 servings)

100Authenticity
  • Eggs7 eggs4 whole eggs for sponge. Separate the other 3: use their 3 whites in the sponge and 3 yolks in buttercream
  • Almond flour100 gFrom blanched almonds
  • Powdered sugar100 g
  • Plain cake flour28 gSift. Use plain soft-wheat cake flour without added starch or leavening.
  • Unsalted butter140 g20g for sponge, melted and clarified; 90g for buttercream, softened at room temperature; 30g for ganache
  • Granulated sugar150 g90g for buttercream and 60g for coffee syrup
  • Water130 ml30ml for buttercream syrup and 100ml for coffee syrup. The 1 teaspoon hot water for dissolving coffee is additional.
  • Instant coffee8 g6g for coffee syrup and 2g for buttercream, dissolved in 1 teaspoon hot water
  • Dark couverture chocolate, about 66% cacao330 g120g for ganache, 40g for the bottom, 160g for glaze and 10g for lettering
  • Heavy cream, at least 35% milk fat76 mlFor ganache
  • Grapeseed oil24 gFor the glaze
  • Salt0.5 gA pinch for the whites
  • Edible gold leaf1 sheetOptional; tear into small pieces

Method

115 min

Make the biscuit Joconde batter

  • 1 Melt 20g unsalted butter in a small saucepan, let stand briefly and reserve only the clear upper portion as clarified butter.
  • 2 Beat 4 whole eggs, 100g almond flour and 100g powdered sugar in a bowl with a hand mixer until pale and doubled in volume.
  • 3 In another bowl, add 0.5g salt to 3 whites and beat to firm peaks.
  • 4 Sift 28g cake flour into the egg batter and fold with a flexible spatula, mix in the clarified butter, then add to the beaten whites and combine without deflating.
  • 5 MeanwhileWhile beating, preheat the oven to 180°C and line a 27cm square baking sheet with parchment.

Ready when

Continue when the batter is glossy and flows slowly.

224 min

Spread thinly in a 27cm square and bake at 180°C for 12 minutes, twice

  • 1 Spread half the batter flat on the baking sheet about 5mm thick and bake at 180°C for 12 minutes.
  • 2 Transfer with its paper to a rack to cool. Bake the remaining batter in the same way.

Ready when

Continue when the surface is lightly golden and springs back to a gentle press.

35 min

Make and cool a strong coffee syrup

  • 1 Heat 100ml water and 60g sugar in a small saucepan over medium heat. When the sugar dissolves, turn off the heat, dissolve 6g instant coffee in it and cool.

Ready when

Continue when the sugar and coffee have dissolved and the syrup is at room temperature.

425 min

Whip yolks with 121°C syrup and add butter and coffee

  • 1 Beat 3 yolks in a bowl with a hand mixer until pale.
  • 2 Heat 30ml water and 90g sugar in a small saucepan and cook to 121°C, checking with a thermometer.
  • 3 While beating the yolks, pour the hot syrup in a thin stream down the bowl edge and beat until cool to make pâte à bombe, yolks whipped with hot syrup.
  • 4 Gradually add 90g softened unsalted butter while beating, then mix in 2g instant coffee dissolved in 1 teaspoon hot water to make crème au beurre, or buttercream.

Ready when

Continue when the cream is smooth, not separated, and spreadable with a flexible spatula.

Caution

Adding butter while the pâte à bombe is warm melts it and causes separation.

520 min

Make ganache and chill until spreadable

  • 1 Finely chop 120g couverture chocolate and put in a bowl.
  • 2 Heat 76ml cream to just below boiling and pour over. Leave for 1 minute, stir from the center to melt, then mix in 30g unsalted butter to make ganache, chocolate combined with heated cream.
  • 3 Refrigerate, stirring every 5 minutes. When creamy, take out to room temperature.

Ready when

Continue when the ganache remains glossy and can be spread thinly with a spatula.

635 min

Cut 18cm squares, coat the bottom with chocolate and layer in a frame

  • 1 Cut 2 squares of 18cm from the cooled sponge and combine the scraps to make a third 18cm square.
  • 2 Melt 40g chocolate over a water bath, spread thinly on one side of 1 intact square and refrigerate for 10 minutes to set.
  • 3 Set an 18cm square frame on a parchment-lined work surface. Put the chocolate-coated sponge inside, chocolate side down. Brush generously with coffee syrup and spread half the buttercream, about 120g, in a level layer 3–4mm thick.
  • 4 Brush one side of sponge sheet 2, made from scraps, with syrup and place syrup side down. Brush the top too and spread the ganache thinly and evenly.
  • 5 Brush both sides of sheet 3 with syrup in the same way, place on top and level the remaining buttercream to 3–4mm thick.

Ready when

Continue when the total height is no more than 3cm and the top is level.

Caution

Cream smeared on the frame’s inner sides prevents cleanly visible layers.

72 hr

Refrigerate for at least 2 hours

  • 1 Refrigerate in the frame for at least 2 hours.

Ready when

Continue when the surface buttercream is firm enough that a finger leaves no mark.

815 min

Unmold and spread a thin chocolate glaze in one stroke

  • 1 Wrap the outside of the frame in a warmed cloth to release it, remove and place the cake on a rack over a tray.
  • 2 Melt 160g chocolate over a water bath, mix in 24g grapeseed oil and cool until warm to the touch.
  • 3 Pour over the cold cake and spread thinly from edge to edge in one stroke with an offset spatula, letting excess run down. Refrigerate for 10 minutes.

Ready when

Continue when the glaze is glossy, 2–3mm thin and no longer sticks to a finger.

Caution

Repeated spreading leaves marks and removes the shine.

915 min

Trim the edges, write “Opéra” and add gold leaf

  • 1 Warm a knife in hot water, wipe dry and trim 0.5cm from all four sides.
  • 2 Melt 10g chocolate over a water bath, transfer to a parchment cornet and write “Opéra” on top. Optionally add 1 small piece of edible gold leaf.
  • 3 Cut into 9 portions, warming and wiping the knife as you go, and refrigerate.

Ready when

The cake is ready when the cut surfaces show straight layers of sponge, cream and ganache.

About this dish

At the Paris pastry house Dalloyau in 1955, Cyriaque Gavillon is said to have made a low, square layered cake. His wife Andrée named it Opéra, likening it to the stage of the Palais Garnier opera house.

Another account points to the Clichy, a cake of sponge, coffee and chocolate at the pastry shop taken over in 1955 by Marcel Bugat, who had married Gavillon’s sister. That cake has also been described as the original. The pastry chef Gaston Lenôtre claimed in the early 1960s that he had invented Opéra himself.

Opéra is still said to be Dalloyau’s best-selling pastry, and it appears in sample questions for France’s national pastry qualification examination.

Chef Authen’s tip

Writing “Opéra” on top was an essential finish in a French culinary encyclopedia’s definition in the 1990s, but the 2007 revision is said to have changed this to something done “sometimes.”
