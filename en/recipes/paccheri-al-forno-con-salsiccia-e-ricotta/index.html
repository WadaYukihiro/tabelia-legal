About this dish

In Naples, paccheri al forno almost invariably appear at festive gatherings and large family meals. Christmas, Easter, weddings and birthdays: this baked pasta appears wherever people gather.

Tomato sauce, sausage, ricotta, mozzarella and Parmigiano form layers in a baking dish. The oven melts the cheeses, and when the edges brown and become fragrant, the dish is brought to the table. Someone takes the first scoop. The cheese stretches. That alone brings cheers.

The skill this pasta asks for is building layers: boiling the paccheri until firm, 4 minutes less than the indicated time, making the sauce and assembling the layers. Once it is in the oven, all that remains is to wait, but neglecting any of those 3 earlier stages changes the entire result.

Chef Authen’s tip

The paccheri are boiled until firm, lifted out 4 minutes before the package time, because they will cook further in the oven. Anyone who thinks they would be better a little softer and boils them for the full time will face a mushy texture when they emerge from the oven. The pasta’s firmness is determined before it enters the oven, and knowing this makes a clear difference to the finished dish.
