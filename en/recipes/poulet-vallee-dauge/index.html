Poulet Vallée d'AugeNormandy Chicken with Cider and Apples

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep25 min Cook1 hr 31 min servings4

A Normandy classic: 1 whole chicken is jointed into 8 pieces, lightly browned in butter, braised with Calvados and dry cider, then thickened with an egg-yolk and cream liaison. Apples, mushrooms and aromatics form its structure; careful reduction and finishing the liaison without boiling determine the flavor. Bone-in chicken savor joins cider acidity, apple sweetness and creamy richness in a main course of 1 whole bird for 4 people.

Method

125 min

Joint the chicken into 8 pieces and prepare for searing

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile the chicken rests for 15 minutes, prepare 3 thyme sprigs and 1 bay leaf, and measure 150 ml chicken stock.
  • 2 MeanwhileWhile the chicken rests for 15 minutes, finely chop 60 g onion.
  • 3 MeanwhileWhile the chicken rests for 15 minutes, wipe 200 g mushrooms with a damp cloth, trim the stem ends, cut large ones lengthwise into 4 and toss with 5 ml lemon juice.
  • 4 Pat the 1200 g whole chicken dry and place breast-side up on a board. Cut the skin at the leg joints, pull the legs outward to dislocate the joints and detach the 2 whole legs.
  • 5 Cut each of the 2 legs at the white joint line into 2 drumsticks and 2 thighs.
  • 6 Remove the 2 wing tips at their joints and reserve for the carcass.
  • 7 Cut the breasts away along either side of the breastbone, leaving the upper wings attached to the 2 breasts. Halve each breast diagonally, making 2 pieces with wing attached and 2 without, for 8 chicken pieces in total.
  • 8 Use kitchen shears to divide the remaining backbone, breastbone and wing tips, plus neck if present, into roughly 5 cm pieces and keep together as the carcass.
  • 9 Season all surfaces of the 8 pieces and carcass with 6 g salt and 1 g black pepper; rest at room temperature for 15 minutes.

Ready when

The chicken is divided into 8 pieces: 2 drumsticks, 2 thighs, 2 breast pieces with wing and 2 without, plus the carcass, all evenly seasoned.

Continue once surface moisture has receded and the meat's color has settled slightly.

Caution

Cut through joints rather than forcing the knife through bone, which can leave bone fragments.

225 min

Brown lightly and dust with flour

  • 1 Heat 30 g butter and 15 ml sunflower oil in a heavy cast-iron pot about 24 cm across over medium heat, until the butter's bubbles become fine.
  • 2 Arrange the chicken skin-side down without overlapping and cook undisturbed over medium heat for 5–6 minutes.
  • 3 Once pale golden, turn and cook the other side for 4 minutes. Work in 2 uncrowded batches, in the order drumsticks, thighs, then breasts; transfer browned pieces to a tray.
  • 4 Brown the carcass in the same pot for 2–3 minutes, turning, then transfer to the tray.
  • 5 Perform singer, dusting with flour and cooking it as a thickening base: return chicken and carcass, sift 15 g flour over through a tea strainer and cook over medium-low heat for 2 minutes, turning with a wooden spoon.

Ready when

The chicken surface is pale gold, or blond, rather than dark brown.

Continue when the flour's whiteness and raw quality disappear and a thin layer of browned deposits forms on the pot bottom.

37 min

Soften onion and deglaze with Calvados

  • 1 Add 60 g chopped onion between the chicken pieces and sauté over medium-low heat for 3–4 minutes, stirring until translucent and its moisture evaporates, without coloring.
  • 2 Turn off the heat briefly and pour 50 ml Calvados down the side of the pot.
  • 3 Return to medium heat and boil for 1–2 minutes, scraping the savory browned layer from the bottom with a wooden spoon, until the sharp alcoholic smell disappears.

Ready when

The onion is translucent without browning.

Continue when the deposits dissolve and color the liquid, with no sharp alcohol smell when smelled close to the pot.

Caution

Do not ignite the alcohol.

427 min

Braise in cider and stock, removing breasts first

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile braising, combine 500 ml water and 5 ml lemon juice in a bowl. Peel 2 apples, core and cut each 1 into 8 wedges, placing them immediately in the lemon water, for 16 wedges total.
  • 2 Pour in 300 ml dry cider and 150 ml chicken stock; add 3 thyme sprigs and 1 bay leaf.
  • 3 Bring to a boil over medium-high heat and skim floating scum with a ladle 3–4 times.
  • 4 Set the lid slightly ajar and simmer gently for 15 minutes, with the surface barely moving.
  • 5 After 15 minutes, use tongs to remove all 4 breast pieces, 2 with wing and 2 without, to a plate and cover with foil.
  • 6 Continue simmering the remaining 2 thighs, 2 drumsticks and carcass over the same low heat for another 10 minutes.

Ready when

A skewer inserted into the thickest thigh releases clear juices.

Continue when the liquid is slightly thickened and reduced to about 2/3 of its initial volume, roughly 300 ml.

510 min

Strain and reduce to 250 ml

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile reducing, melt 15 g butter in a frying pan over medium heat and spread in 200 g mushrooms. Cook untouched for 2 minutes, then for 3–4 minutes more, turning occasionally until dry and browned at the edges; season with 1 g salt and remove from the heat.
  • 2 MeanwhileWhile reducing, whisk 1 egg yolk and 50 ml thick crème fraîche, cultured cream, smoothly in a small bowl to make a liaison, the egg-yolk and cream mixture used to thicken sauce.
  • 3 Remove the remaining 2 thighs and 2 drumsticks to the plate with the 4 breast pieces and cover with foil.
  • 4 Remove and discard carcass, thyme and bay leaf with tongs.
  • 5 Strain the liquid into a measuring jug through a chinois, a fine conical sieve, or other fine strainer; press the retained onion lightly with the back of a ladle to extract liquid.
  • 6 Wipe fat and browned deposits from inside the empty cast-iron pot with paper towels and return the strained liquid.
  • 7 Reduce over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, carefully skimming surface fat with a ladle.

Ready when

The liquid is reduced to about 250 ml and nearly no film of fat remains on the surface.

Continue when the mushrooms' released moisture has evaporated completely and they smell toasted.

616 min

Finish with liaison, reheat chicken and sauté apples

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile reducing the cream, lift the 16 apple wedges from the lemon water and dry thoroughly with paper towels.
  • 2 MeanwhileWhile reducing the cream, melt 25 g butter in another frying pan over medium heat. Once foaming, arrange the 16 wedges without overlapping and cook for 3 minutes per side, turning gently only once with a spatula, for 6–7 minutes total until pale caramel on both sides; add 1 g salt and turn off the heat.
  • 3 MeanwhileWhile reheating the chicken, pick the leaves from 4 parsley sprigs and chop coarsely.
  • 4 Add the remaining 150 ml thick crème fraîche to the reduced liquid and cook over medium heat for 4–5 minutes, stirring, until lightly thickened.
  • 5 Reduce to low heat and temper the liaison with 1 ladleful of hot sauce, about 60 ml, at a time, whisking in 3 additions.
  • 6 Return the warmed liaison to the pot and heat on the lowest setting for 2–3 minutes, constantly drawing a wooden spoon across the bottom.
  • 7 Turn off the heat and season with 5 ml lemon juice, 2 g salt and 0.5 g black pepper.
  • 8 Return the sautéed mushrooms and all 8 chicken pieces; reheat over the lowest heat for 3–4 minutes, spooning sauce over them.

Ready when

The sauce coats the back of a wooden spoon and a finger drawn through it leaves a trail that does not run.

Continue when the chicken is hot to the center and the sauce has never boiled.

The apple edges are amber and a skewer slips through, but the wedges hold their shape.

Caution

Boiling after adding the egg yolk will split the sauce.

76 min

Plate warm with apples and parsley

  • 1 Fill 4 deep plates with hot water for 1–2 minutes, discard it and dry with a clean cloth.
  • 2 Arrange 2 chicken pieces per person in the center of the 4 warm plates, combining drumstick, thigh, breast with wing and breast without wing so each type is distributed.
  • 3 Divide mushrooms among the 4 plates around the chicken and ladle sauce over everything.
  • 4 Arrange 4 sautéed apple wedges per person in a fan along the rim.
  • 5 Grind 0.5 g black pepper over the 4 plates and scatter with chopped parsley.

Ready when

The dish is complete when the sauce spreads thinly on the plates and the outlines of chicken and apples remain visible.

About this dish

Pays d'Auge is a hilly area extending from eastern Calvados to northern Orne in Normandy. Apple orchards dot its pastures, and it is also famous for cheeses such as Camembert, Pont-l'Évêque and Livarot.

When the autumn apple harvest begins, cider is made here, then distilled into Calvados and matured in barrels. Vallée d'Auge in a dish name indicates preparation with the area's apples, cider, Calvados and cream; it is used for fish and veal as well as chicken.

This dish of a whole chicken jointed and braised has long served gatherings around Normandy family tables. Local restaurants still list it as a representative regional specialty from autumn through winter.

Chef Authen’s tip

Normandy has a custom called the trou normand, the Norman hole. During a long meal, a small glass of Calvados is drunk to make a figurative hole in the stomach for the next course. Restaurants now often serve it as apple sorbet topped with Calvados. The return of apple aroma halfway through the courses is part of the rhythm of a local feast.
