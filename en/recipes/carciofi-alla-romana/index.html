Carciofi alla RomanaRoman-Style Braised Artichokes

Ingredients (2 servings)

  • Mammola artichokesRemove outer leaves, cut off the top 1/3 and soak in lemon water4 pieces
  • Mentuccia (or peppermint)Finely chop10 pieces
  • Flat-leaf parsleyFinely chop5 sprigs
  • GarlicFinely chop2 cloves
  • Extra virgin olive oil80 ml
  • Dry white wine100 ml
  • Salta little

Method

115 min

Trim the artichokes

  • 1 Remove the outer leaves from 4 artichokes.
  • 2 Cut off the top 1/3.
  • 3 Peel the stems, leaving about 3 cm attached.
  • 4 Keep in lemon water as you work.

Caution

Rubber gloves are recommended because your hands may blacken. Immerse cut surfaces in lemon water immediately.

25 min

Make the herb paste

  • 1 Finely chop 10 mentuccia leaves.
  • 2 Finely chop 5 parsley sprigs.
  • 3 Finely chop 2 garlic cloves.
  • 4 Mix the chopped herbs, garlic, a pinch of salt and 2 tsp olive oil into a paste.
35 min

Stuff with the paste

  • 1 Use your fingers to open the spaces between the artichoke leaves.
  • 2 Pack the herb paste between the leaves.
42 min

Arrange for braising

  • 1 Stand the artichokes in a deep pot with the heads down and stems up.
530 min

Braise the artichokes

  • 1 Pour the remaining olive oil, 100 ml white wine and 100 ml water into the pot.
  • 2 Cover and heat over medium heat for 5 minutes.
  • 3 Reduce to low heat and braise for 25 minutes.

Ready when

Proceed when a bamboo skewer slides easily into the stems.

62 min

Serve with the cooking juices

  • 1 Check the tenderness of the stems with a bamboo skewer.
  • 2 Plate with the cooking juices.

Ready when

They are ready when the skewer slides in easily.

About this dish

Spring in Rome begins when artichokes appear in the markets. The rounded mammola variety, available from March to May, is an emblematic vegetable of Lazio’s spring and appears as a seasonal sign in both home kitchens and trattoria chalkboard menus.

Carciofi alla romana are braised artichokes long enjoyed as Roman home cooking. Compared with carciofi alla giudia, fried and opened like flowers, this version has a gentler, more domestic character.

They may be served warm or slightly cooled as an antipasto or contorno. In springtime Rome they sit beside lamb or fish dishes, passed down as a plate that lightens the table with seasonal bitterness and the aroma of herbs.

Chef Authen’s tip

Artichokes are stood upside down in the pot for more than appearance. Steam and oil carry the aromas of mint, garlic and parsley packed between the leaves into the interior. Laying them on their sides may be easier, but an easy position will not necessarily make the aromas do their work.
