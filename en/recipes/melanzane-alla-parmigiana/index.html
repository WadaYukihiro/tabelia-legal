Authentic Melanzane alla Parmigiana

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep36 min Cook1 hr 10 min servings4

Salted eggplant, fried until tender and layered with tomato sauce, mozzarella and Parmigiano-Reggiano — a rich baked dish from Southern Italy.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Eggplants900 g
  • Tomato passata700 ml
  • Fior di latte mozzarella250 g
  • Parmigiano Reggiano80 g
  • Fresh basil12
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Frying oil1000 ml
  • Extra virgin olive oil30 ml
  • Salt14 g

Method

145 min

Draw out the eggplant’s moisture

Trim the tops from 900g eggplant and slice lengthwise 7mm thick. Sprinkle with 10g salt, arrange in a colander and let stand for 40 minutes, until beads of moisture appear on the surface and the slices bend softly.

28 min

Prepare the cheese and aromatics

Cut 250g mozzarella into 1cm cubes and drain in a colander. Grate 80g Parmigiano-Reggiano. Lightly crush the garlic clove.

324 min

Make the tomato sauce

Heat the olive oil with the garlic over low heat. Once fragrant, add the passata, 4g salt and 4 basil leaves and simmer for 20 minutes, until slightly reduced and the acidity has mellowed. Remove the garlic.

48 min

Pat the eggplant dry

Rinse the eggplant briefly under running water and pat thoroughly dry with paper towel, until no surface moisture remains — this prevents the oil from spattering.

518 min

Fry the eggplant

Heat the frying oil to 170°C and fry the eggplant in batches. Fry both sides for 2–3 minutes until lightly golden and tender, then drain on a rack.

612 min

Layer the dish

In a baking dish, layer tomato sauce, eggplant, mozzarella, Parmigiano-Reggiano and basil. Repeat for about 3 layers, finishing with tomato sauce and Parmigiano-Reggiano.

76 min

Heat the oven

Preheat the oven to 190°C. The layers should reach evenly to the edges of the dish, with the cheese spread thinly on top.

830 min

Bake until golden

Bake at 190°C for 30 minutes, until the top is golden and the sauce bubbles lightly at the edges.

910 min

Rest before serving

Rest for 10 minutes, until the layers settle and the slices hold their shape when cut.

About this dish

Southern Italy’s summer markets pile high with glossy eggplant, ripe tomatoes and basil. Melanzane alla Parmigiana layers and bakes these seasonal ingredients together, and it’s loved above all around Campania and Sicily.

One theory traces the name to its use of Parmigiano; another says it comes from a Sicilian word for shutter slats, since the layered eggplant slices resemble the boards of a shutter. As the use of tomato and cheese spread through the 18th and 19th centuries, something close to today’s form took shape.

Though it contains no meat, it carries enough presence to appear on a festive table too. Vegetables, dairy and tomato sauce layered together symbolise the richness of southern Italian home cooking.

Chef Authen’s tip

Parmigiana isn’t “eggplant gratin.” It’s a dish where fried eggplant, tomato and cheese need to settle and come together as one. Cut it fresh from the oven and every layer you built collapses. Waiting is as much a part of the method here as anything on the stove.
