Method

18 min

Heat the citrus peel and anise in oil

  • 1 Wash the lemon and orange, and use a vegetable peeler to remove thin strips of colored peel. Remove the white pith and weigh the amounts listed in the ingredients.
  • 2 Put the olive oil for the dough and the measured peel in a small saucepan over low heat. When the peel starts to color lightly, add all the anise seeds and heat for about 30 seconds.

Ready when

Turn off the heat and continue when the peel is lightly golden and the anise is fragrant.

220 min

Cool the infused oil

  • 1 Take the saucepan off the heat and leave for about 20 minutes to cool to room temperature.

Ready when

Continue when the oil and the bottom of the pan are no longer hot.

312 min

Mix the dough with flour and sherry

  • 1 Strain the cooled oil through a fine strainer to remove the peel and anise seeds.
  • 2 Mix 300g cake flour, salt and toasted white sesame seeds in a bowl, then add the fino sherry and strained oil.
  • 3 Knead by hand for about 8 minutes.

Ready when

Continue when no dry flour remains and the dough is smooth and does not stick to your hands.

430 min

Rest the dough

  • 1 Cover the dough with plastic wrap and rest at room temperature for 30 minutes.

Ready when

Continue after 30 minutes, when the dough yields slightly without resistance when pressed.

530 min

Roll thinly and overlap the corners

  • 1 Lightly dust the work surface with the 20g flour reserved for dusting. Divide the dough into manageable portions and roll to 2mm thick.
  • 2 Cut into 7cm squares and position each as a diamond. Fold the left and right corners toward the center, overlapping them, and press firmly with your fingers.
  • 3 Gather the scraps, roll to the same thickness and shape them too.

Ready when

Continue when the overlapping corners stay attached and both ends remain open.

635 min

Fry in small batches until golden

  • 1 Use a pot with a base 16cm across and at least 10cm deep. Add frying olive oil to a depth of 3cm; adjust by depth rather than pouring in the entire amount.
  • 2 Heat to 170°C and add enough pastries to leave gaps between them without overlapping. Fry for about 2–3 minutes per batch, turning them during cooking.
  • 3 Lift out with a slotted ladle and drain on paper towels. Bring the oil back to 170°C before frying the remaining pastries in the same way.

Ready when

Continue when both sides and the folds are golden and the surface is crisp.

Tips

Increasing the servings increases the number of batches and total time. Keep the oil depth, temperature, batch size and frying time per batch unchanged.

710 min

Coat with honey

  • 1 Put the honey and water in a small saucepan 12–16cm across and warm over low heat, stirring. Turn off the heat when it starts to boil.
  • 2 Add the fried pastries in small batches and turn to coat both sides. Lift out with tongs, let excess liquid drain and arrange on a plate. Coat the rest in the same way.

Ready when

Continue when both sides of every pastry have a thin, glossy coating of honey.

810 min

Let cool briefly and serve

  • 1 Leave the pastries on the plate for about 10 minutes to cool, then divide into the required servings.

Ready when

They are ready when they are no longer very hot and the honey coating remains on the surface.

About this dish

Andalusian pestiños are sweet fried pastries served during Holy Week and at Christmas. Some are coated with honey, others with sugar and cinnamon; their appearance varies from place to place and household to household, even under the same name.

In the southern port city of Cádiz, people gather to share them at an event called the Pestiñada. This free celebration marks the start of Carnival: people meet in a square to eat pestiños while listening to singing and the sounds of guitars, bandurrias and drums.

Chef Authen’s tip

Anise seeds are the small spice seeds known as matalahúva in Spain. Some pestiño recipes remove the seeds after infusing the oil; others mix them into the dough. The proportions also vary between households.
