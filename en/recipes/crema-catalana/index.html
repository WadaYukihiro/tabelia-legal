Crema catalanaCatalan Custard with Caramelized Sugar

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep15 min Cook15 min Rest3 hr servings4

Milk and egg yolks are infused with lemon and cinnamon and cooked into a custard in a saucepan. This Catalan dessert combines a cold centre with a thin lid of caramel.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Milk600 ml
  • Egg yolks4 yolks
  • Granulated sugar80 g for the custard; 30 g for the surface110 g
  • Cornflour25 g
  • Cinnamon stick1 stick
  • Lemon peel (Japanese-grown, unwaxed)5 g

Method

110 min

Infuse the milk with citrus and cinnamon

  • 1 Thinly peel 5 g of lemon zest, avoiding the white pith.
  • 2 Put 500 ml of milk, 1 cinnamon stick and the lemon peel in a saucepan and warm over low heat until bubbles appear around the edges.
  • 3 Turn off the heat, cover and leave for 5 minutes.

Ready when

Continue when the milk has taken on the aromas and has not boiled over.

25 min

Combine the egg yolks and starch with the milk

  • 1 Mix 4 egg yolks and 80 g of sugar in a bowl.
  • 2 In a separate bowl, dissolve 25 g of cornflour in the remaining 100 ml of cold milk, then mix into the yolks.
  • 3 Strain the infused milk and gradually add it to the yolk mixture.

Ready when

Continue when there are no lumps of starch and the mixture is an even pale yellow.

310 min

Thicken the custard in the saucepan

  • 1 Return the mixture to the saucepan and cook over moderate-low heat for 5–8 minutes, scraping the bottom as you stir.
  • 2 When it thickens and one or two small bubbles rise, stir over low heat for 1 minute, then turn off the heat.
  • 3 Divide evenly among 4 shallow heatproof dishes.

Ready when

Continue when the custard is smooth and the marks left by the whisk disappear slowly.

43 hr

Chill in the dishes

  • 1 Press cling film directly onto the surface. Once the initial heat has dissipated, refrigerate for 3 hours.

Ready when

Continue when the custard is chilled to the centre and has set.

55 min

Caramelise a thin sugar crust

  • 1 Remove the cling film, gently blot any surface moisture with kitchen paper, and sprinkle 30 g of sugar in a thin, even layer over the 4 dishes.
  • 2 On a heatproof work surface, move a kitchen blowtorch over the sugar to caramelise only the topping, then leave for 1 minute.

Ready when

It is ready when the surface forms a thin amber sheet that breaks with a spoon.

About this dish

Crema catalana is also known as crema de Sant Josep. The name comes from the custom of eating it on 19 March, Saint Joseph's Day, which is also celebrated as Father's Day.

In Catalonia, sweets are linked to seasonal festivities. This custard is both a dessert bearing the region's name and a celebratory sweet on the spring calendar. Tracing its names reveals the relationship between food and annual celebrations.

Traditionally, a heated iron was used to caramelise the sugar on the surface. The combination of soft egg-and-milk custard and a thin layer of caramel has been passed down along with the dessert's names.

Chef Authen’s tip

Crema catalana is also called crema cremada. Names referring to a saint, a region and the characteristic burnt surface all belong to the same sweet. Different names on a menu may therefore refer to the same dessert.
