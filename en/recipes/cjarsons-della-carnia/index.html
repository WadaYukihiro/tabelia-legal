Cjarsons della CarniaCarnia Cjarsons

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep1 hr 5 min Cook2 hr 23 min Rest2 hr servings4

Sweet-and-savory filled pasta traditionally made around Easter and for celebrations in Carnia’s mountain villages. A difficult dough incorporating boiled potatoes is rolled extremely thin and filled with potatoes, fresh ricotta, raisins, candied citron, unsweetened cocoa, cinnamon and wild herbs. Browned butter, grated smoked ricotta, sugar and cinnamon finish a dish whose filling varies by household, earning it the description “a dish with a hundred recipes.”

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Yellow-fleshed potatoes (for the dough)240 gRaw, unpeeled weight. Boil whole in their skins, peel and pass through a sieve while hot to yield about 190 g. Dense yellow-fleshed potatoes are easier to handle
  • Type 00 soft wheat flour135 gUse 105 g in the dough and 30 g for dusting during rolling
  • Egg yolk1 pieceBinds the dough; keep refrigerated until just before use
  • Butter (for the dough)10 gSoften at room temperature and knead into the dough to improve its stretch
  • Potatoes (for the filling)190 gRaw, unpeeled weight. Boil in their skins and pass through a sieve to yield about 150 g. Cool completely before use
  • Fresh cow's milk ricotta150 gDrain in a sieve in the refrigerator for 2 hours to about 120 g
  • Fresh spinach80 gA substitute for Carnia’s wild herbs, erbe spontanee. Boil, squeeze firmly and mince very finely, yielding about 25 g after squeezing
  • Fresh mint8 gPick and finely chop only the leaves; a central cjarsons aroma
  • Lemon balm4 gFinely chop only the leaves
  • Marjoram3 gFinely chop only the leaves
  • Flat-leaf parsley8 gFinely chop only the leaves; for the filling
  • Onion50 gMince very finely; sauté in butter until sweet and add to the filling
  • Butter (for sweating the onion)15 gFor sautéing the onion in the filling
  • Sultana raisins30 gSoak in lukewarm water for 20 minutes, squeeze firmly and roughly chop
  • Candied citron25 gCut into 3 mm dice
  • Unsweetened cocoa powder5 gFor the filling; sift before adding
  • Dry amaretti biscuits25 gCrush finely; absorbs moisture from the filling and adds a bitter-almond aroma
  • Fine dried breadcrumbs15 gThe quantity required to adjust the filling moisture to a consistency suitable for enclosing
  • Granulated sugar (for the filling)15 g
  • Ground cinnamon (for the filling)1.5 g
  • Nutmeg0.5 gGrate just before use
  • Lemon zest1 pieceUnwaxed; grate only the yellow zest
  • Grappa15 mlAdd to the sautéed onion and boil to drive off the alcohol
  • Smoked ricotta60 gGrate over to finish; use 15 g per 1 plate
  • Butter (for the browned butter dressing)60 gBrown it, then divide it all evenly among the 4 plates
  • Granulated sugar (for finishing)8 gMix with cinnamon and sprinkle after plating
  • Ground cinnamon (for finishing)1 gMix with sugar and sprinkle over the plate
  • Fine salt4 gUse 2 g in the dough and 2 g in the filling
  • Coarse salt (for the cooking water)40 gAdd to 4 L cooking water at 10 g/L
  • Black pepper1 gFreshly ground; for the filling

Method

12 hr

Drain the ricotta and soak the raisins

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the ricotta drains) Cut 25g candied citron into 3mm dice.
  • 2 MeanwhilePut 25g amaretti in a plastic bag and crush finely with a rolling pin.
  • 3 Drain 150g fresh ricotta in a paper-lined sieve in the refrigerator for 2 hours.
  • 4 Soak 30g raisins in lukewarm water at 40°C for 20 minutes, squeeze firmly, and roughly chop.

Ready when

The ricotta should stop releasing moisture and hold its shape when lifted.

It should weigh about 120g.

The raisins should be soft and plump to the center.

Caution

Insufficiently drained ricotta loosens the filling and can tear the dough after it is filled.

Tips

In Carnia, every household has a different filling, so this is called “a dish with a hundred recipes.” Careful preparation of the dried ingredients is the only common foundation.

245 min

Boil and sieve the potatoes, dividing them for dough and filling

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the potatoes boil) Wash 80g spinach and very finely chop 50g onion.
  • 2 MeanwhileSet out 10g dough butter to soften; take the egg yolk from the refrigerator just before using it.
  • 3 Put 430g unpeeled potatoes in cold water and bring to a boil. Cook over medium heat for 25–35 minutes after boiling.
  • 4 Peel and sieve while hot. Weigh out 190g for the dough and 150g for the filling, and spread out the filling potatoes to cool.

Ready when

A skewer should slide into the center without resistance.

The sieved potatoes should have no pieces or lumps.

They should be weighed into 190g for the dough and 150g for the filling.

Caution

Boiling peeled potatoes makes them absorb water, resulting in sticky dough and unpredictable extra flour requirements.

Sieving after cooling produces stickiness and makes both dough and filling heavy.

Tips

Boiling in skins, peeling, and sieving yields about 80% of the raw weight. This recipe assumes 430g raw potatoes yields about 340g sieved potatoes.

312 min

Make the potato dough while warm

  • 1 Cut and combine the warm dough potatoes with 10g butter, 2g salt, and 70g flour in that order to make a crumbly mixture. Add 1 egg yolk and knead for 3–4 minutes.
  • 2 Gradually add 35g flour, bring together into a smooth dough, and wrap in plastic wrap.

Ready when

The surface should be even and uncracked, springing back slowly when pressed.

The dough should not cling stickily to the work surface.

Caution

Overkneading releases potato starch, making the dough rubbery and liable to tear when rolled.

Adding all the flour at once makes it too stiff to roll extremely thin.

430 min

Rest the dough in the refrigerator

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the dough rests) Finely chop the leaves of 8g mint, 4g lemon balm, 3g marjoram, and 8g flat-leaf parsley.
  • 2 MeanwhileBring water for the spinach to a boil in a small pot, then continue preparing the filling in steps 5 and 6.
  • 3 Rest the wrapped dough in the refrigerator for 30 minutes and keep refrigerated until step 8.

Ready when

The dough should spring back slowly under a finger and its surface should not be dry.

Tips

Potato dough has weaker gluten than egg dough, and a longer rest does not improve its stretch. 30 minutes is enough.

522 min

Prepare the filling aromatics and herbs

  • 1 Cook the onion in 15g butter over low heat for 8–10 minutes without browning. Add 15ml grappa, bring to a boil over medium heat to drive off the alcohol smell, and let cool.
  • 2 Boil the spinach for 1 minute, transfer to cold water, squeeze firmly, finely chop, and squeeze again.

Ready when

The spinach should contain almost no moisture and separate loosely.

The onion should be translucent, not brown, with no sharp alcohol smell when sniffed closely.

The onion should have cooled to room temperature.

Caution

Never ignite alcohol. Boil to remove the alcohol while retaining the aroma.

Adding hot onion to ricotta makes it separate and become watery.

618 min

Combine the sweet-and-savory filling

  • 1 Before mixing, check that the ricotta and spinach are not releasing water; drain further if needed. Cut and combine the cooled filling potatoes with the ricotta without kneading, then mix in the herbs, spinach, and onion with its juices.
  • 2 Add the raisins, citron, amaretti, 5g sifted cocoa, 15g granulated sugar, 1.5g cinnamon, 0.5g nutmeg, the grated zest of 1 lemon, 2g salt, and 1g black pepper. Mix until the color is even.
  • 3 Add 15g breadcrumbs in 2 additions, then chill in step 7 before checking whether the filling holds its shape.

Ready when

A spoonful should hold its shape without any liquid seeping out.

Caution

Kneading with a rubber spatula releases potato starch and turns the filling gluey.

Loose filling tears the dough during shaping and leaks out while boiling. If it still releases liquid after chilling, do not use it to fill the pasta: make it again with thoroughly drained ingredients.

730 min

Chill the filling to firm it

  • 1 Meanwhile(While chilling) Lightly dust a tray with a little of the 30g flour reserved for dusting.
  • 2 Press plastic wrap directly onto the filling and refrigerate for 30 minutes.

Ready when

The filling should be cold and firm, with no moisture rising when pressed with the back of a spoon.

Caution

Filling the pasta at room temperature lets the filling soften and spread into the edges, preventing sealing.

Tips

Chilled, firm filling is easier to weigh and roll into 10g portions and less likely to tear the extremely thin dough.

835 min

Roll very thinly and cut 48 rounds

  • 1 Divide the dough into 4 portions. Wrap the unused portions and return them to the refrigerator. Roll only the portion you have taken out on a floured work surface to 1mm thick.
  • 2 Cut with an 8cm round cutter and cover with a cloth. Reroll the scraps as well, to make 48 rounds in total.

Ready when

The dough should be thin enough to faintly reveal the outline of a finger underneath.

There should be 48 moist rounds under the cloth, without drying.

Caution

Too much dusting flour stops the edges bonding and makes them open during boiling.

Kneading scraps firmly makes the dough tight and prone to tearing as it is rolled.

940 min

Fill and seal 48 cjarsons

  • 1 Place 10g filling on each round, lightly moisten the edge, fold into a half-moon, and push out the air as you seal. Pinch the edge into pleats.
  • 2 Arrange without overlapping on a floured tray. Whenever a tray is full, cover and refrigerate it. Once all 48 are filled, take out only 12 at a time for cooking.

Ready when

Every edge should be fully sealed and stay closed when pressed.

No air pockets should remain inside.

Caution

Trapped air expands during boiling and bursts the pasta.

Too much water makes the edges slide instead of sticking, causing them to open. Cover and refrigerate any leftover filling, then cook it to a center temperature of at least 75°C for 1 minute and eat it that day.

1014 min

Warm the plates, brown the butter and grate smoked ricotta

  • 1 Meanwhile(While browning the butter) Bring 4L water with 40g coarse salt to a boil in a large pot.
  • 2 Warm 4 plates in an oven at 70°C.
  • 3 Finely grate 60g smoked ricotta and mix the finishing 8g granulated sugar with 1g cinnamon.
  • 4 Brown 60g butter in a small heavy frying pan over medium-low heat for 4–6 minutes. Turn off the heat when hazelnut-colored and set the pan on a damp cloth. Divide the browned butter equally among 4 small heatproof containers.

Ready when

The butter should be clear amber with a toasted nut aroma.

The cooking water should be boiling vigorously across its surface.

The 4 plates should feel warm to the touch.

Caution

Once browned butter begins coloring, it can blacken and turn bitter within tens of seconds. Do not delay removing it from the heat.

Smoked ricotta hardens in a hot frying pan, so always use it on the plates.

Tips

The cooking water has 10g/L salt. Because the filling is sweet, the salted water defines the dish’s overall flavor.

1122 min

Cook 12 at a time and finish each plate separately

  • 1 Keep the water just below boiling, around 90°C, with the surface barely trembling (frèmere). Add 12 cjarsons and gently release them from the bottom once.
  • 2 Use 2 minutes after they float as a guide, then measure the center of the filling in the largest piece with a food thermometer. Continue cooking until the center has remained at or above 75°C for 1 minute. Lift out with a slotted ladle, drain, and transfer to a warm plate.
  • 3 Return one plate’s portion of browned butter to the pan, rewarm over low heat for 10 seconds, and pour over. Sprinkle with 15g smoked ricotta and 1/4 of the cinnamon sugar, and serve immediately. Cook and finish the remaining cjarsons in batches of 12 in the same way.

Ready when

The dough and filling should be cooked through, and the center of the filling must have remained at or above 75°C for 1 minute.

Caution

A vigorous boil tears the dough and releases the filling into the water. Keep just below boiling.

Do not judge doneness by floating or translucent dough alone. Continue cooking if the center temperature is too low.

About this dish

Carnia is a mountainous area in northern Friuli-Venezia Giulia on the Austrian border. Its villages were historically isolated valley by valley, and its cooking evolved slightly differently in each valley.

Cjarsons are filled pasta handed down in those valleys, with sweet and salty elements sharing the filling. Raisins, candied citrus, cinnamon and cocoa join potatoes, ricotta and herbs. They are thought to reflect spices and dried fruit once carried across the Alps by itinerant merchants becoming established in mountain home cooking.

Today they remain on menus at osterias around Tolmezzo throughout the year. The finish is browned butter, smoked ricotta, sugar and cinnamon. Being served without deciding whether they are sweet or savory is the dish’s true character.

Chef Authen’s tip

Cjarsons filling varies by household and valley. Combining all the ingredients used is said to produce a list of more than 40, and neighboring valleys can insist that each other’s version is wrong. The only common feature is putting sweet and salty ingredients together in a single filling.
