Bœuf BourguignonBeef Bourguignon

Difficulty★★☆☆☆ Prep15 min Cook3 hr 42 min Rest12 hr servings4

Beef bourguignon is Burgundy’s emblematic beef stew in red wine. In the traditional method, beef chuck is marinated overnight in red wine, then braised in that same marinade for nearly 3 hours, developing deep richness and complex acidity. Using slab bacon for lardons and a medium-bodied Pinot Noir available in Japan for Burgundy wine still reproduces the essential technique and flavor. The proper finish is a garniture bourguignonne of pearl onions and mushrooms.

Method

115 min

Marinate the beef and aromatic vegetables in wine

  • 1 Cut 900 g beef chuck into 3–4 cm cubes.
  • 2 Peel 3 carrots and cut into irregular chunks.
  • 3 Peel 2 onions and cut into wedges.
  • 4 Finely chop 3 garlic cloves.
  • 5 Trim the stem ends from 300 g mushrooms and halve any large ones.
  • 6 Tie 3 thyme sprigs, 2 bay leaves and 4 parsley stems together with kitchen string to make a bouquet garni (a bundle of herbs).
  • 7 Put the beef, carrots, onions and bouquet garni in a storage container and pour in 750 ml red wine, submerging them well.
212 hr

Marinate overnight to absorb flavor

  • 1 Cover with plastic wrap and marinate in the refrigerator overnight (12 hours).

Ready when

The meat is marinated when the wine has penetrated all over and its color has turned reddish purple.

310 min

Dry and flour the beef

  • 1 Set a colander over a bowl and separate the marinade from the solids.
  • 2 Reserve the marinade.
  • 3 Separate the beef, carrots, onions and bouquet garni. Pat the beef dry with kitchen paper, then lightly coat with 30 g cake flour.
420 min

Brown the lardons and beef

  • 1 Fry 150 g lardons (pork belly cut into batons) in a heavy pot over medium heat; remove when the fat is translucent and lightly colored.
  • 2 Add 20 g butter and 30 ml neutral oil to the same pot, arrange the beef without overlapping and sear without moving it.
  • 3 Remove once deeply browned on all sides.
  • 4 Work in several batches.
515 min

Sauté the vegetables and complete the braising liquid

  • 1 Add the marinated carrots and onions to the same pot and sauté over medium heat for 5 minutes.
  • 2 Once softened, add 30 g tomato paste and fry for 1 minute, then return the beef and lardons to the pot.
  • 3 Pour in the reserved marinade and boil over medium heat for 3–4 minutes to drive off the sharp smell of alcohol.
  • 4 Add 300 ml beef stock, the bouquet garni, 1 tsp salt and 1 tsp black pepper.
62 hr 45 min

Braise the beef until tender

  • 1 Cover and turn the heat to low; braise for 2 hours 30 minutes–3 hours, keeping the surface at a gentle simmer.
  • 2 Skim off floating fat every 30 minutes.

Ready when

Proceed when a bamboo skewer enters the beef with almost no resistance and its fibers fall apart tenderly.

718 min

Cook the pearl onions and mushrooms

  • 1 Meanwhile(While the beef braises) cook the pearl onions and mushrooms.
  • 2 Melt 20 g butter in a frying pan over low heat, add the pearl onions, cover and gently steam-braise for 10 minutes without browning.
  • 3 Add the mushrooms, remove the lid and sauté over medium heat for 5 minutes; turn off the heat once lightly colored.

812 min

Reduce the sauce and return the ingredients

  • 1 Remove the beef and discard the bouquet garni.
  • 2 Reduce the liquid over medium heat for about 5 minutes until it leaves a thin coating on the back of a wooden spoon. Return the beef, lardons, pearl onions and mushrooms and warm through.
  • 3 Adjust with salt and black pepper and scatter with finely chopped parsley.

About this dish

Beef bourguignon began as farmers’ practical knowledge: tough cuts from “working cattle” used for agricultural labor in Burgundy were slowly braised in local wine until tender. Burgundy is known for red wines made from Pinot Noir, and its local wine has long formed the base of this braise.

The dish gained international recognition as an emblem of French cooking after it featured prominently in Julia Child’s cookbook published in America in 1961.

Even today, both Burgundy households and bistros prepare it for Sunday family gatherings and special occasions. Its demanding preparation, including the overnight wine marinade, preserves its character as a “special braise” made slowly over a weekend.

Chef Authen’s tip

The traditional method uses the red-wine marinade directly in the braise rather than discarding it. The meat’s flavor and aroma have already dissolved into the marinade; pouring it away is like throwing away kombu and bonito flakes after making stock, along with their savor, saying they have already done their job. The previous night’s preparation exists for the next day’s pot; it is not merely a ritual.
