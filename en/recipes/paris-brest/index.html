Paris-Brest

Difficulty★★★★★ Prep1 hr 40 min Cook35 min Rest2 hr 20 min servings4

A wheel-shaped choux pastry said to have been created to commemorate the bicycle race between Paris and Brest. A homemade praline of toasted almonds and hazelnuts is blended into crème mousseline made with butter and pastry cream, then piped inside. One of the great Parisian pastries, it combines 3 techniques: raising choux pastry, caramelizing praline and emulsifying cream.

Method

110 min

Prepare the praline

  • 1 Line a baking tray with baking paper.
  • 2 Dry-toast 40 g almonds and 40 g hazelnuts in a frying pan; remove when the skins are lightly browned and the nuts smell toasted.
28 min

Coat the nuts in caramel

  • 1 Put 75 g granulated sugar in the same pan and heat over medium heat without stirring.
  • 2 When it is uniformly dark amber, turn off the heat, add the toasted nuts and quickly coat them.
33 min

Cool and set the praline

  • 1 Spread the coated nuts on the baking paper and cool without overlapping.
420 min

Let the praline set completely

  • 1 Leave for 20 minutes until completely cool and hard.

58 min

Make praline paste

  • 1 Continue processing, scraping down the sides as needed.

Ready when

The praline (a paste of nuts and caramel) is ready when smooth.

Caution

Break the cooled, set caramelized nuts into rough pieces and process into a paste in a food processor (about 3–5 minutes).

63 min

Prepare the oven and tray

  • 1 Begin preheating the oven to 200°C.
  • 2 Line a baking tray with baking paper and draw a circle about 18 cm in diameter on its reverse.
78 min

Bring the choux base to the boil

  • 1 Put 125 ml water, 125 ml milk, 110 g butter for the choux, 3 g salt for the choux and 5 g granulated sugar for the choux in a saucepan over medium heat.
  • 2 When the butter is fully melted and the liquid boils, turn off the heat.
85 min

Add flour and form the dough

  • 1 Add all 140 g cake flour for the choux at once and mix quickly with a wooden spoon.
  • 2 Return to low heat and work for 1–2 minutes until the dough comes away from the base as a single mass and a thin film forms on the bottom.

Ready when

Continue when the dough pulls away as one mass and a thin film forms on the pan bottom.

910 min

Add eggs to finish the choux

  • 1 Transfer the dough to a bowl and stir for 2–3 minutes to cool slightly.
  • 2 Add 4 beaten eggs for the choux in 4–5 additions, mixing thoroughly each time.

Ready when

Continue when the dough falls slowly from a lifted spoon in an inverted triangle.

107 min

Pipe a choux ring

  • 1 Put the dough in a piping bag fitted with a 15 mm round nozzle and pipe a ring along the drawn circle.
  • 2 Pipe 1 more circle inside and 1 outside to create a thick ring.
  • 3 Scatter 30 g sliced almonds over the surface.
1135 min

Bake the choux ring

  • 1 Bake at 200°C for 35 minutes.
  • 2 Do not open the oven during baking.
  • 3 Doing so makes it collapse.

Caution

It is baked when risen, deeply golden and firmly set.

1230 min

Cool the choux ring

  • 1 Place the tray on a rack and cool completely.
135 min

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile the choux cools, split 1/2 vanilla pod lengthwise and scrape out the seeds.

Caution

145 min

Whisk the yolks and sugar

  • 1 Whisk 4 egg yolks with 70 g granulated sugar for the pastry cream in a bowl until pale and thick.
  • 2 Sift in 30 g cornstarch and mix.
158 min

Cook the pastry cream

  • 1 Gradually strain in the warm milk while mixing.
  • 2 Return to the saucepan over medium heat and cook, stirring constantly.
  • 3 Once thickened and bubbling, stir for about 1 minute more; turn off the heat when it forms a thick cream.
161 hr 30 min

Chill the pastry cream

  • 1 Spread in a shallow tray, press plastic wrap against the surface and refrigerate for 1 hour 30 minutes.
178 min

Make the mousseline cream

  • 1 Put 200 g room-temperature butter for the mousseline in a bowl and beat with a whisk until creamy.
  • 2 Add the chilled pastry cream and mix thoroughly until smooth.
  • 3 Add all the praline and mix further; it is ready when evenly light brown.
1810 min

Pipe cream into the choux

  • 1 Cut the cooled choux ring in half horizontally.
  • 2 With a round nozzle, pipe crème mousseline (custard enriched with butter) onto the bottom half, then replace the top.
193 min

Finish with powdered sugar

  • 1 Sift 10 g powdered sugar over the whole pastry through a tea strainer to finish.

About this dish

In 1910, Louis Durand, a pastry chef along the route, is said to have invented this pastry to commemorate the long-distance Paris–Brest–Paris cycling event linking Paris with the Breton port of Brest. Its round ring imitates a bicycle wheel, giving the pastry its name.

A large ring of piped and baked choux is filled with crème mousseline made from butter and pastry cream, into which homemade toasted-almond and hazelnut praline is blended. Throughout the 20th century, this construction was refined as a staple of Parisian pastry shops.

Today, most leading Paris pâtisseries present their own Paris-Brest as a signature item, competing over praline proportions and roasting levels. Although inspired by a bicycle race, the pastry now represents Parisian pastry culture itself.

Chef Authen’s tip

The Paris–Brest–Paris cycling event began in 1891 as a grueling race covering almost 600 km each way. The round Paris-Brest ring depicts the very bicycle wheel that travels this distance. The event continues every 4 years as one of the oldest amateur long-distance races, meaning both the pastry and the original wheels have completed more than 1 century.
