Terrine de Foie GrasDuck Foie Gras Terrine

Raw duck foie gras is deveined, packed into a terrine mold, scented with Armagnac and baked in a water bath. This cold appetizer rests in the refrigerator for 72 hours after cooking before being sliced 1cm thick.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (8 servings)

100Authenticity
  • Fresh raw duck foie gras, unsliced500 gWeigh 500 g fresh raw foie gras from an unsliced lobe or attached lobes. Keep refrigerated until preparation; this route starts with fresh liver. A nominal retail lobe weight does not replace weighing.
  • Fine salt7 g7g for 500g foie gras
  • Freshly ground black pepper1 gMix with the salt
  • Armagnac10 ml2 teaspoons; sprinkle over the foie gras in the mold

Method

15 min

Weigh and check the fresh foie gras

  • 1 Weigh 500 g fresh raw foie gras from an unsliced lobe or attached lobes and place on a tray. Keep refrigerated until the next step.

Ready when

Continue when the fresh liver is chilled and 500 g has been weighed.

21 hr

Bring the foie gras to room temperature

  • 1 Take the foie gras out of the refrigerator. Place on a paper-towel-lined tray and leave at room temperature for 60 minutes.

Ready when

Continue when it yields like modeling clay and bends without cracking.

Caution

Cold liver is firm and can crack when the veins are removed.

325 min

Open the liver pieces and remove the veins

  • 1 If the large and small lobes are attached, separate them at their join with your fingers. Lay the lobe or pieces flat with the inner surface facing up.
  • 2 Use the back of a small knife or your fingers to open the largest piece over the central Y-shaped main vein.
  • 3 Slide a finger beneath the vein and pull it toward you, removing the main vein and its branches together.
  • 4 Remove the second vein about 5mm below the main one in the same way, then bring the opened liver back together by hand.
  • 5 Devein any smaller piece in the same way, avoiding breaking it up too much.

Ready when

Continue when no red vein lines remain inside the liver and it stays in large pieces.

Tips

The small lobe has veins intertwined in two layers and breaks easily. Broken pieces will join together when pressed into the mold.

410 min

Season, pack into the terrine and sprinkle with Armagnac

  • 1 Mix 7 g salt with 1 g black pepper and sprinkle evenly on both sides of all the liver pieces.
  • 2 Put the largest piece, smooth outer side down, in a terrine mold of 0.7 L capacity. Add the remaining pieces on top.
  • 3 Press with your palm to fill the corners and expel air, then level the surface.
  • 4 Sprinkle 10ml Armagnac over the surface and press plastic wrap directly against the liver.

Ready when

Continue when the corners are filled without gaps and the surface is level.

Tips

Packing tightly without air gaps reduces the amount of fat released during cooking.

524 hr

Refrigerate for 24 hours

  • 1 Refrigerate the covered mold for 24 hours.

Ready when

Continue after 24 hours.

615 min

Preheat to 110°C and prepare the water bath

  • 1 Preheat the oven to 110°C.
  • 2 While the oven preheats, boil 1 L water in a kettle.
  • 3 Take the mold out of the refrigerator, remove the plastic wrap and put on the terrine lid.
  • 4 Place the mold in a deep ovenproof container and pour boiling water around it to half its height.

Ready when

Continue immediately when the oven has reached 110°C and the water reaches halfway up the mold.

750 min

Cook in the water bath until the center reaches 75°C

  • 1 Place the water bath on the middle oven rack and bake for 35 minutes.
  • 2 Continue baking until the center reaches 75°C, then remove.

Ready when

Continue when the center temperature has reached 75°C.

872 hr 30 min

Cool briefly and refrigerate for 72 hours

  • 1 Take the mold out of the water bath and leave at room temperature for 30 minutes to cool slightly, without removing the fat collected on top.
  • 2 Keep the mold covered and refrigerate for 72 hours.

Ready when

Continue when the surface fat has set white and 72 hours have passed.

91 hr 10 min

Unmold and cut into 1cm slices

  • 1 Take the mold out of the refrigerator 1 hour before eating.
  • 2 Dip the mold in hot water for a few minutes to warm its sides, then invert onto a cutting board to unmold.
  • 3 Cut into slices about 1cm thick and plate about 50g per person.

Ready when

The terrine is ready when the slices hold together with a smooth cut surface.

About this dish

From October through March, producers in Gimont and Eauze in the southwestern department of Gers sell duck and goose foie gras, carcasses and poultry at marchés au gras, or “fat markets.” The market in Samatan runs year-round, with additional special markets before Christmas in December.

One reason duck foie gras production spread in southwestern France was corn, introduced in the 16th century. The 2011 specifications for the protected geographical indication “Southwestern duck for foie gras” required at least 95% of the fattening feed to be corn grown in the southwest.

A 2006 French law recognized foie gras as part of the country’s protected cultural and gastronomic heritage. In a 2022 survey, 75% named foie gras among their favorite festive foods, ahead of smoked salmon and bûche de Noël.

Chef Authen’s tip

Removing the blood vessels from foie gras is sometimes called dénerver, “remove nerves.” The CIFOG industry guide calls this incorrect: foie gras has no nerves to remove, only blood vessels, so the operation should be called déveiner, “remove veins.”
