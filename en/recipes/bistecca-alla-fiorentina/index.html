Authentic Bistecca alla Fiorentina

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep5 min Cook25 min servings4

A thick-cut T-bone seared hot, rested, then finished simply with salt and olive oil — Tuscany’s signature steak. The thickness of the cut, the sear and the resting time decide everything.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Thick-cut beef T-bone steak1200 g
  • Coarse sea salt8 g
  • Black pepper2 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • Lemon1

Method

145 min

Bring the steak to temperature

Remove the T-bone from the refrigerator 45 minutes before cooking and pat the surface dry. It should be 4–5cm thick, with the chill just barely off the centre.

23 min

Prepare the finishing touches

Cut half a lemon into wedges. Set aside the coarse salt and black pepper for finishing.

36 min

Get the pan smoking hot

Heat a cast-iron or grill pan over high heat for at least 5 minutes, until a drop of water sizzles instantly and a faint haze of smoke rises.

412 min

Sear the surface hard

Stand the steak on its fat edge for 2 minutes, then sear one side for 5 minutes and the other for 5 minutes, until deeply coloured and the meat releases naturally when moved.

54 min

Finish from the bone side

Stand the steak bone-side down for 2–3 minutes, until the internal temperature reaches 48–50°C and it gives gently when pressed. Remove from the heat.

610 min

Rest the steak

Transfer to a board, season with half the coarse salt and rest for 10 minutes, until no juices run when cut and the seared colour has settled.

75 min

Carve and finish

Cut the meat from the bone and slice 1.5cm thick against the grain. Season the cut surface with the remaining salt and pepper, drizzle with the olive oil and serve with the lemon.

About this dish

Florence, spread along the Arno, is a Renaissance city of art — but it’s also a town of meat cookery, supported by Tuscany’s cattle-raising culture centred on the Chianina breed. Bistecca alla Fiorentina, a thick bone-in steak, symbolises that regional character.

The name bistecca is said to derive from the English “beef steak,” spreading through contact with British travellers who visited Florence in the 18th and 19th centuries. Today it’s treasured as the dish that represents Tuscany’s festive table, served in trattorias around San Lorenzo and grill houses out in the countryside.

This dish sits at the point where rural cattle-raising, urban dining-out culture, and the custom of sharing meat at the table all meet. Behind a plate that looks bold and rustic is the core value of Tuscan cooking: trust in the ingredient itself.

Chef Authen’s tip

Bistecca isn’t just “a big steak.” It only becomes fiorentina when thickness, bone, high heat and resting time all come together. Cook a thin cut of meat beautifully and you’ve made a good steak — but you haven’t made Florence’s dish.
