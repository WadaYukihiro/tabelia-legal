Bistecca alla FiorentinaFlorentine T-Bone Steak

Difficulty★★★★☆ Prep5 min Cook25 min servings4

Tuscany’s emblematic meat dish: a thick T-bone seared over high heat, rested, then finished with salt and olive oil. Thickness, browning and resting time determine its flavor.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Thick-cut beef T-bone steak4–5 cm thick; remove from the refrigerator 45 minutes before cooking1200 g
  • Coarse sea salt8 g
  • Black pepper2 g
  • Extra virgin olive oil20 ml
  • LemonCut into wedges; serve alongside if desired1 piece

Method

145 min

Bring the steak toward cooking temperature

  • 1 Take the 1200 g beef T-bone steak out of the refrigerator 45 minutes before cooking and thoroughly pat the surface dry.

Ready when

The steak should be 4–5 cm thick and its center should have lost some of its chill.

23 min

Prepare the finishing ingredients

  • 1 Cut 0.5 lemon into wedges.
  • 2 Prepare 8 g coarse salt and 2 g black pepper for finishing.
36 min

Heat the cooking surface thoroughly

  • 1 Heat a cast-iron frying pan or grill pan over high heat for at least 5 minutes.

Ready when

Proceed when it is hot enough for a drop of water to burst immediately and a light wisp of smoke to rise.

412 min

Sear the outside thoroughly

  • 1 Stand the T-bone steak upright and sear the fatty edge for 2 minutes.
  • 2 Then cook 1 side for 5 minutes and the other for 5 minutes.
  • 3 The surface should be deeply browned and release naturally when you move the meat.
54 min

Cook toward the center from the bone

  • 1 Stand the steak bone-side down and cook for 2–3 minutes.

Ready when

Remove from the heat when the center reaches 48–50°C and still feels softly springy when pressed.

610 min

Rest the meat

  • 1 Transfer the steak to a board, sprinkle with half the coarse salt and rest for 10 minutes.
  • 2 This prevents juices from running out when sliced; proceed once the seared surface has settled.
75 min

Slice and finish

  • 1 Remove the meat from the bone and slice across the grain into 1.5 cm-thick pieces.
  • 2 Sprinkle the cut surfaces with the remaining coarse salt and black pepper, then drizzle with 20 ml extra virgin olive oil in a thin stream.
  • 3 Serve with the lemon to finish.

About this dish

Florence stretches along the Arno and is both a city of Renaissance art and a city of meat cookery supported by Tuscany’s livestock culture, including Chianina cattle. Bistecca alla fiorentina is a thick bone-in steak that symbolizes this connection to place.

The word bistecca is said to derive from the English “beef steak.” One theory holds that it spread through contact with British travelers visiting Florence in the 18th–19th centuries. Today, trattorias around San Lorenzo and grill restaurants outside the city serve it as an emblem of Tuscany’s celebratory table.

The dish arises from the intersection of rural livestock farming, urban dining culture and the custom of sharing meat at the table. Behind its imposing appearance lies the Tuscan value of placing trust in the ingredients themselves.

Chef Authen’s tip

Bistecca is not simply a “large steak.” Only thickness, bone, fierce heat and resting time together make it fiorentina. A thin cut grilled elegantly may be a good steak, but it is not this Florentine dish.
