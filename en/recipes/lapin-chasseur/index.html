Lapin ChasseurHunter-Style Rabbit

Difficulty★★★☆☆ Prep25 min Cook1 hr 10 min servings4

Rabbit is browned, then braised with mushrooms, white wine and tomatoes in the chasseur style. The browned juices on the bottom of the pot enrich the sauce, while the mushrooms are cooked separately to evaporate their moisture and build layers of flavor.

IngredientsMethodAbout this dish

Ingredients (4 servings)

  • Bone-in rabbit piecesMeat processed and inspected for food use. Thaw frozen meat in the refrigerator from the day before1000 g
  • Button mushrooms250 g
  • Shallots80 g
  • Garlic1 clove
  • Chopped tomatoes200 g
  • Dry white wine200 ml
  • Unsalted chicken stock250 ml
  • Unsalted butter30 g
  • Vegetable oil15 ml
  • Plain flour15 g
  • Thyme2 pieces
  • Bay leaf1 piece
  • Flat-leaf parsley5 g
  • Salt6 g
  • Black pepper0.5 g

Method

120 min

Prepare the meat and aromatics

  • 1 Pat 1000 g rabbit dry, remove any remaining bone fragments and season all over with 4 g salt and 0.5 g black pepper.
  • 2 Peel and finely chop 80 g shallots and 1 garlic clove.
  • 3 Clean 250 g mushrooms, trim their stems and cut into 4 pieces each. Pick and chop the leaves from 5 g parsley.

Ready when

Proceed when no moisture is pooling on the meat and the aromatics are finely and evenly chopped.

215 min

Brown the rabbit

  • 1 Dust the rabbit lightly with 15 g plain flour.
  • 2 Heat 15 ml vegetable oil and 10 g butter in a heavy pot over medium heat. Brown the rabbit on both sides in 2 batches, without overlapping the pieces.
  • 3 Transfer the browned meat to a plate.

Ready when

Proceed when the meat is golden and the flavorful deposits on the bottom of the pot are not burnt black.

310 min

Deglaze with white wine

  • 1 Reduce the heat under the same pot to low and sauté the shallots and garlic for 3 minutes.
  • 2 Add 200 ml white wine, scrape up the browned deposits with a wooden spoon and reduce by about half over medium heat.
  • 3 Add 200 g chopped tomatoes, 250 ml chicken stock, 2 thyme sprigs and 1 bay leaf. Return the rabbit and any juices on the plate to the pot.

Ready when

Proceed when the sharp smell of alcohol has gone and the browned deposits have dissolved.

440 min

Braise the rabbit until tender

  • 1 MeanwhileWhile it braises, melt 10 g butter in a frying pan over medium heat, spread out the mushrooms and sauté for 6–8 minutes. Turn off the heat when their moisture has evaporated and they are lightly browned.
  • 2 Once simmering, lower the heat, leave the lid slightly ajar and simmer for about 40 minutes. Turn the meat after 20 minutes.
  • 3 Use a clean probe thermometer to measure the center of a thick piece and confirm that it stays at 75°C or above for at least 1 minute.

Ready when

Proceed when a skewer enters the meat near the bone with little resistance and the center has remained at 75°C or above for at least 1 minute.

55 min

Combine the mushrooms and sauce, then serve

  • 1 Remove the thyme and bay leaf and add the sautéed mushrooms to the pot.
  • 2 Reduce uncovered for 5 minutes and add the remaining 2 g salt.
  • 3 Turn off the heat and melt in the remaining 10 g butter. Divide the meat and mushrooms among 4 warm deep plates, spoon over the sauce and scatter with parsley.

Ready when

It is ready when the sauce lightly coats the meat and gives the mushrooms a glossy sheen.

About this dish

Chasseur means “hunter-style” and is also the name of a French sauce prepared with mushrooms and other ingredients. Lapin chasseur is a warming stew that showcases rabbit.

At an autumn family table, it can be served in deep plates as the main course. Chasseur preparations differ according to region and cook; this version uses a combination of mushrooms, white wine and tomatoes.

Chef Authen’s tip

Although its name is lapin, the rabbit in this recipe is meat processed for human consumption. French culinary terminology distinguishes it from lièvre, which refers to hare.
